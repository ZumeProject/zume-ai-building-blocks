# Portuguese (Portugal) Translation Reference Guide (pt_PT)

**Source File:** `po_files/zume-pt_PT.po`
**Purpose:** Reference guide for maintaining consistent spiritual terminology in Portuguese (Portugal) translations
**Date Analyzed:** 2026-01-16

---

## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Portuguese translations as used in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy uses European Portuguese (pt_PT) with accessible, practical language tailored for Portuguese speakers in a simple church/house church context. The approach emphasizes action-oriented discipleship over theological abstraction, using informal peer-level language to facilitate multiplication and networking.

### Key Language Characteristics

**Formality Register:** The translation consistently uses "você" (informal/neutral you) rather than "tu" (formal you), making it accessible and conversational while maintaining respect. This choice reflects modern European Portuguese usage in relational ministry contexts.

**European vs Brazilian Portuguese:** This translation is specifically European Portuguese (pt_PT), as indicated in the file header. While many terms are shared across Portuguese variants, there are distinct characteristics in vocabulary, spelling, and phrasing that distinguish it from Brazilian Portuguese (pt_BR).

## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus
- **Portuguese:** "Jesus"
- **Notes:** Consistently translated with standard Portuguese spelling. Uses "de Jesus" (of Jesus) for possessive forms.
- **Examples:**
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands by going AND staying" → "Aprenda a obedecer aos mandamentos de Jesus, indo E ficando"
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "Como seguir a Jesus"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "O que é um seguidor de Jesus?"
  - "How do I obey Jesus and help others become followers with me?" → "Como eu obedeço a Jesus e ajudo outros a serem discípulos comigo?"
  - "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you" → "Ensinando outros a obedecer e seguir a Jesus com você"

### Christ
- **Portuguese:** "Cristo"
- **Notes:** Primarily appears in compound phrases
- **Examples:**
  - Found in references to "body of Christ" (corpo de Cristo)

### God
- **Portuguese:** "Deus"
- **Notes:** Used consistently throughout the translation
- **Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Deus usa pessoas comuns"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Ouvir e obedecer a Deus"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Ser discípulo significa que ouvimos a Deus e obedecemos a Deus"
  - "What do you learn about God?" → "O que você aprende sobre Deus?"
  - "God makes plans, and He expects us to make plans, too" → "Deus faz planos e espera que também façamos planos"
  - "God uses ordinary people doing simple things to make a big impact" → "Deus usa pessoas comuns que fazem coisas simples para provocar um grande impacto"
- **Variations:**
  - "God's Kingdom" → "reino de Deus" or "Reino"
  - "God's voice" → "voz de Deus"
  - "God's Story" → "História de Deus"

### Holy Spirit
- **Portuguese:** "Espírito Santo"
- **Notes:** Always capitalized. Uses formal theological term.
- **Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "Peça ao Espírito Santo de Deus que guie seu tempo juntos"
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time, together" → "Peça ao Espírito Santo de Deus que guie seu tempo, juntos"

### Spirit / Spiritual
- **Portuguese:**

- **Portuguese:** "discípulo" (singular), "discípulos" (plural)
- **Related terms:** "discípulos multiplicadores" (multiplying disciples)
- **Examples:**

## Translation Principles Observed

1. **Accessibility over Formality:** Uses "você" instead of "tu" for accessible, conversational tone
2. **Action-Oriented Language:** Emphasizes verbs and doing (fazer, compartilhar, treinar, obedecer)
3. **Capitalization Patterns:**
   - Deity terms: Capitalized (Deus, Jesus, Espírito Santo)
   - Spiritual concepts as titles: Often capitalized (Respiração Espiritual, Pessoa de Paz)
   - General spiritual terms: Lowercase (igreja, discípulo, oração)
4. **Compound Term Strategy:** Many English compound concepts kept together (e.g., "Discipulado de patinhos")
5. **Tool Names:** Often capitalized and treated as proper nouns (Lista de Cem, Ciclo de Oração)
6. **Acronyms:** Kept in English where widely understood (SOAPS, BLESS)

## European Portuguese vs Brazilian Portuguese Notes

While this translation is specifically European Portuguese (pt_PT), many core spiritual terms are shared across Portuguese variants. Key European Portuguese characteristics observed:

1. **Vocabulary choices:** "estagiário" (trainee) is more European; Brazilian might use "estagiário" or "treinando"
2. **Spelling patterns:** Standard European Portuguese orthography
3. **Pronoun usage:** "você" is common to both, though Brazilian Portuguese uses it more universally
4. **Verb conjugations:** Follow European Portuguese patterns
5. **Terminology consistency:** Spiritual/theological terms are generally consistent across variants (discípulo, igreja, oração, etc.)
