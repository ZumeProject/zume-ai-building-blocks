## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Yoruba translations as used in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy employs **Christian missionary vocabulary** that has been established through centuries of Bible translation and missionary work among the Yoruba people of Nigeria and West Africa. Yoruba is a tonal Niger-Congo language with a rich spiritual vocabulary, having adapted traditional religious terms to Christian contexts while maintaining distinct Christian terminology.

**Key Linguistic Features:**
- **Tonal Language:** Yoruba uses three tones (high, mid, low) marked with diacritics (á, a, à) that change word meanings
- **Vowel Harmony:** Seven oral vowels (a, e, ẹ, i, o, ọ, u) with distinctions crucial for meaning
- **Nasalization:** Marked with subdot on vowels (ẹ, ọ, ṣ, n̄)
- **Agglutinative Structure:** Words built with prefixes and suffixes (e.g., ọmọ-ẹhin = disciple, literally "one who follows behind")
- **Latin Script with Diacritics:** Modern standard orthography uses tone marks and special characters

**Cultural Context:**
- **Traditional Religion:** Yoruba traditional religion (Òrìṣà worship) has influenced vocabulary; many terms adapted for Christian use
- **Islamic Influence:** Some Arabic loanwords exist due to Islamic presence in Yoruba regions
- **Christian Missions:** Anglican, Baptist, and Methodist missions established Christian vocabulary from 19th century
- **Nigerian Context:** Yoruba is spoken by 40+ million people primarily in southwestern Nigeria, Benin, and Togo

## Core Deity Terms

### God
- **Yoruba:** Ọlọrun / Olohun (variant spelling)
- **Literal Meaning:**
  - Ọlọrun = "Owner of Heaven" or "Lord of Heaven"
  - From: Ol- (owner/master) + ọrun (heaven/sky)
- **Tone Marks:**
  - Ọlọrun (standard with tones)
  - Olohun (alternative spelling in translations)
  - Olorun (simplified without middle tone)
- **Traditional Context:** Pre-Christian Yoruba term for Supreme Being, adopted by missionaries
- **Usage:** Primary term for God in Christian contexts, distinct from òrìṣà (traditional deities)
- **Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Ọlọrun Nlo Awọn eniyan lasan"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Gbo ati gboran si Olorun"
  - "Spiritual Breathing is Hearing and Obeying God" → "Nípa tẹ̀mí mimi ni gbọ ati gbigbọran si Ọlọrun"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Jije ọmọ-ẹhin tumọ si pe a gbọ lati ọdọ Ọlọrun a gbọràn sí Ọlọrun"
  - "What do you learn about God?" → "Kkilo ko nipa Olohun?"
  - "God makes plans, and He expects us to make plans, too" → "Ọlọrun ṣe awọn eto, ati pe o nireti pe ki a ṣe awọn ero, paapaa"
  - "Have you ever stopped to think about where God's Kingdom... isn't?" → "Njẹ o ti duro lati ronu nipa ibi ti Ijọba Ọlọrun... kii ṣe?"
  - "Sharing God's Story" → "Pínpín Ìtàn Ọlọrun"
- **Related Forms:**
  - Ọlọrun (God - nominative)
  - ti Ọlọrun (of God - genitive)
  - lati ọdọ Ọlọrun (from God)
  - si Ọlọrun (to God)

### Jesus
- **Yoruba:** Jesu
- **Tone Pattern:** Mid-low (Je-su)
- **Grammatical Notes:**
  - Proper noun, no prefix variation
  - Derived from mission translation tradition
- **Etymology:** From Portuguese/English mission translations (Jesus)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **Jesu** - standard form in Yoruba Bible translations (Bíbélì Mímọ́)
- **Examples:**
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands by going AND staying" → "Kọ ẹkọ bi o ṣe le ṣègbọràn si awọn aṣẹ Jesu nipa lilọ ATI duro"
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "Bi a ṣe le tẹle Jesu"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "Kini ọmọ-ẹhin Jesu kan?"
  - "What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?" → "Kini awọn iṣẹ ṣiṣe ti ọmọlẹhin Jesu kan?"
  - "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you" → "Kikọ awọn miiran lati gbọ ati tẹle Jesu pẹlu rẹ"
  - "Speaking to people YOU KNOW about Jesus" → "Sọrọ si awọn eniyan O MO nipa Jesu"
  - "Speaking to people YOU DON'T KNOW about Jesus" → "Sọrọ si awọn eniyan O MAA ṢE MỌ nipa Jesu"
  - "Becoming more like Jesus... Together" → "Di bi Jesu... Lapapo"
  - "Learn a simple pattern of making not just one follower of Jesus but entire..." → "Kọ ẹkọ nipa ona kan ti o rọrun lati ṣe omo ehin ti kii ṣe okan ọmọ-ẹhin Jesu..."
- **Related Forms:**
  - Jesu (Jesus - nominative)
  - ti Jesu (of Jesus)
  - nipa Jesu (about Jesus)
  - bi Jesu (like Jesus)
  - awọn aṣẹ Jesu (Jesus' commands)

### Holy Spirit
- **Yoruba:** Ẹmi Mimọ Ọlọrun / Ẹmi Mimọ
- **Literal Meaning:**
  - Ẹmi = Spirit, breath, soul
  - Mimọ = Holy, pure, sacred
  - Ọlọrun = God
  - Combined: "God's Holy Spirit" or "Holy Spirit"
- **Tone Marks:**
  - Ẹmi (with subdot for open vowel)
  - Mimọ (holy - with tone marks)
- **Etymology:**
  - Ẹmi: Yoruba traditional concept of spirit/life force, adapted for Christian Trinity
  - Mimọ: From root mọ́ (clean, pure) with reduplication
- **Translation Strategy:** Distinctly Christian term for Third Person of Trinity, separate from traditional ẹmi concepts
- **Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "Beere lọwọ Ẹmi Mimọ Ọlọrun lati darí akoko rẹ papọ"
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time, together" → "Beere lọwọ Ẹmi Mimọ Ọlọrun lati ṣe itọsọna akoko rẹ, papọ"
  - Context: "Beere lọwọ Ẹmi Mimọ lati darí akoko rẹ papọ" (Ask Holy Spirit to lead your time together)
  - Context: "Pe Ẹmi Mimọ lati dari akoko rẹ papọ" (Call on Holy Spirit to lead your time together)
- **Usage Notes:**
  - Can be used with or without Ọlọrun (of God)
  - Always uses Ẹmi (not emi) - the subdot is significant
  - Emphasizes divine guidance and personhood

### Spirit / Spiritual

- **Grammatical Notes:**

  - ti ẹmi (spiritual life/matters)
  - idagbasoke ẹmi (spiritual growth)
  - aje ẹmi (spiritual economy)

### Lord
- **Yoruba:** Olúwa / Oluwa
- **Literal Meaning:**
  - Ol- (owner, master, lord)
  - -uwa (existence, being)
  - Combined: "Master/Lord"
- **Tone Marks:**
  - Olúwa (with high tone on -ú-)
  - Oluwa (simplified spelling)
- **Traditional Context:** Traditional Yoruba title for master/lord, adopted for Christian use
- **Usage:** Christian title for Jesus/God; also used in secular contexts for "master"
- **Examples:**

- **Related Forms:**
  - Olúwa (Lord - nominative)
  - ti Oluwa (of the Lord)
  - Oúnjẹ Alẹ́ Olúwa (Lord's Supper - lit. "Lord's Evening Meal")

### Kingdom
- **Yoruba:** Ijọba
- **Literal Meaning:**
  - I- (noun prefix)
  - jọba (to reign, rule as king)
  - Combined: "Kingdom, realm, reign"
- **Etymology:** From ọba (king, ruler) with verbal noun formation
- **Traditional Context:** Yoruba kingdoms (Oyo, Ife, etc.) used this term; adapted for "Kingdom of God"
- **Examples:**
  - "Eyes to See Where the Kingdom Isn't" → "Oju lati Wo Nibiti Ijọba naa ko si"
  - "Have you ever stopped to think about where God's Kingdom... isn't?" → "Njẹ o ti duro lati ronu nipa ibi ti Ijọba Ọlọrun... kii ṣe?"
  - Context: "Aje ti Ijọba Ọlọrun" (Economy of God's Kingdom)
- **Related Forms:**
  - Ijọba (kingdom - noun)
  - Ijọba Ọlọrun (Kingdom of God)
  - ti ijọba (of the kingdom)
  - ọba (king)

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
- **Yoruba:** Ọmọ-ẹhin (singular), Awọn ọmọ-ẹhin (plural)
- **Literal Meaning:**
  - Ọmọ = child, young one, follower
  - ẹhin = back, behind, rear
  - Combined: "one who follows behind" (lit. "child of the back/rear")
- **Grammatical Notes:**
  - Hyphenated compound: ọmọ-ẹhin
  - Plural formed with awọn (plural marker): Awọn ọmọ-ẹhin
- **Tone Marks:**
  - Ọmọ (with subdots on both vowels)
  - ẹhin (with subdot on ẹ)
- **Etymology:** Indigenous Yoruba compound for follower/student
- **Usage:** Standard term for disciple/student in Christian contexts; distinct from akẹkọọ (general student)
- **Frequency:** Very common term throughout materials (100+ contexts)
- **Examples:**
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "Itumọ ti o rọrun ti ọmọ ẹ̀yìn ati Ijo"
  - "Disciples and the Church" → "Awọn ọmọ-ẹhin ati awọn Ìjọ"
  - "What's a Disciple?" → "Kini Ọmọ-ẹhin?"
  - "Being A Disciple" → "Jije Ọmọ-ẹhin"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Jije ọmọ-ẹhin tumọ si pe a gbọ lati ọdọ Ọlọrun a gbọràn sí Ọlọrun"
  - "Ask yourself, what does my disciple need" → "Beere lọwọ ara rẹ, kini ọmọ-ẹhin mi nilo"
- **Compound Forms:**
  - "1 trained multiplying disciple per 5,000 in the United States" → "1 koni ọmọ-ẹhin isodipupo fun 5,000 ni Amẹrika"
  - "1 trained multiplying disciple per 50,000 globally" → "1 oṣiṣẹ isodipupo ọmọ-ẹhin fun 50,000 agbaye"
  - "To saturate the world with multiplying disciples in our generation" → "Láti mú kí àwọn ọmọ ẹ̀yìn pọ̀ sí i nínú ayé ní ìran tiwa"
  - "Training Cycle for Maturing Disciples" → "Ipilẹṣẹ Ikẹkọ fun idagbasoke Awọn ọmọ-ẹhin"

## Church Terms

### Church
- **Yoruba:** Ile ijọsin (full form), Ìjọ (short form)
- **Literal Meaning:**
  - Ile = house, building
  - ijọsin = worship, religion
  - Combined: "house of worship"
  - Ìjọ = congregation, assembly
- **Grammatical Notes:**
  - Ile ijọsin (literally "worship house")
  - Ìjọ (from ijọ = to gather/assemble)
  - Plural: Awọn ijọsin / Awọn Ìjọ
- **Tone Marks:**
  - Ìjọ (with grave accent on I)
  - ijọsin (worship)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **Ile ijọsin** for physical/institutional church and **Ìjọ** for congregation/body of believers - dual terminology reflects Yoruba Christian usage
- **Examples:**
  - "Church" → "Ile ijọsin"
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "Itumọ ti o rọrun ti ọmọ ẹ̀yìn ati Ijo"
  - "Disciples and the Church" → "Awọn ọmọ-ẹhin ati awọn Ìjọ"
  - "Churches Needed" → "Orúkọ Ìjọ"
  - "Churches Reported" → "Awọn ijọsin royin"
  - "Always report new simple churches that you are starting" → "Nigbagbogbo jabo awọn ile ijọsin ti o rọrun tuntun ti o bẹrẹ"
  - "Churches" → "Ìjọ"
  - "Always Part of Two Churches" → "Nigbagbogbo Apakan ti Awọn Ijo meji"
- **Compound Terms:**
  - "Simple church" → "ile ijọsin ti o rọrun" / "Ìjọ ti o rọrun"
  - "2 simple churches per 5,000 people in the United States" → "Awọn ile ijọsin ti o rọrun 2 fun eniyan 5,000 ni Amẹrika"
  - Context: "Can a 3/3 Group be counted as a Simple Church?" → "Njẹ Ẹgbẹ 3/3 ni a le ka si Ile-ijọsin Rọrun kan?"
- **Usage Notes:**
  - rọrun (simple) follows the noun
  - ti o rọrun (that is simple - relative clause)
  - Dual meaning: building (ile ijọsin) vs. community (ìjọ)

- **Literal Meaning:**

- **Grammatical Notes:**

  - No plural form typically used
- **Etymology:** Indigenous Yoruba compound created by missionaries to translate "good news"
- **Usage:** Standard Christian term for the Gospel; distinct from traditional oral histories (itan)
- **Examples:**

### Bible / Scripture

## Linguistic and Cultural Notes

### Tone System

Yoruba is a **tonal language** with three distinct tones that change word meanings:

**Tone Types:**
- **High tone (á):** Marked with acute accent
- **Mid tone (a):** Unmarked (default)
- **Low tone (à):** Marked with grave accent

**Examples of Tone Importance:**
- ọkọ (husband) vs. oko (farm) vs. ọkọ̀ (vehicle)
- igba (calabash) vs. igbá (200) vs. ìgbà (time/season)
- ẹmi (I/me) vs. Ẹmi (Spirit) vs. ẹ̀mí (breath)

**In Spiritual Terms:**
- Ọlọrun (God) - specific tone pattern
- Ẹmi Mimọ (Holy Spirit) - subdots and tones crucial
- ọmọ-ẹhin (disciple) - compound with specific tones

### Vowel System

Yoruba has **seven oral vowels** with important distinctions:

**Vowel Types:**
- **a** - open central [a]
- **e** - close-mid front [e]
- **ẹ** - open-mid front [ɛ] (with subdot)
- **i** - close front [i]
- **o** - close-mid back [o]
- **ọ** - open-mid back [ɔ] (with subdot)
- **u** - close back [u]

**Subdot Significance:**
- ẹ vs. e: Different vowel sounds (Ẹmi vs. emi)
- ọ vs. o: Different vowel sounds (Ọlọrun vs. olori)
- Ṣ: Represents [ʃ] sound (sh)

### Traditional Religious Context

**Òrìṣà Worship Influence:**

Traditional Yoruba religion has significantly influenced Christian vocabulary:

**Adapted Terms:**
- **Ọlọrun** - Pre-Christian Supreme Being, now Christian God
- **Ẹmi** - Traditional life force/spirit, now Holy Spirit
- **Iran** - Traditional vision/revelation, now prophetic vision
- **Adura** - Traditional prayer/supplication, now Christian prayer

**Distinction from Traditional Deities:**
- Ọlọrun (Christian God) ≠ òrìṣà (traditional deities like Ṣango, Obatala, Yemoja)
- Christian terminology emphasizes monotheism vs. traditional polytheistic pantheon
- Missionaries carefully distinguished Christian concepts from òrìṣà worship

**Christian-Specific Innovations:**
- **Iribomi** (baptism) - New compound for Christian practice

- **Ọmọ-ẹhin** (disciple) - Adapted traditional follower concept

### Islamic Influence

While less prominent than in Hausa, some **Arabic loanwords** exist:

**Limited Arabic Borrowings:**
- Most spiritual vocabulary is indigenous Yoruba or missionary creations
- Unlike Hausa or Swahili, Yoruba Christian terminology avoids heavy Arabic use
- Reflects Christian missionary preference for indigenous terms

**Contrast with Hausa:**
- Hausa uses: Littafi Mai Tsarki (Arabic-influenced "Holy Book")
- Yoruba uses: Bibeli Mimọ (English borrowing + indigenous "holy")

### Yoruba Orthography

**Standard Writing System:**
- **Latin script** with diacritical marks
- **Tone marks:** á (high), a (mid), à (low)
- **Subdots:** ẹ, ọ, ṣ for specific sounds
- **Nasalization:** n̄ (rarely marked in modern texts)

**Orthographic Variations in Translation:**
- Ọlọrun / Olohun / Olorun (different tone marking conventions)
- Bibeli / Bíbélì (with/without tone marks)
- Adura / Àdúrà (full vs. partial tone marking)
- ọmọ-ẹhin / omo eyin (hyphenation variations)

**Standardization Challenges:**
- Multiple orthographic traditions exist
- Mission translations may use different conventions
- Modern standard tends toward full tone marking

### Translation Consistency Patterns

**Compound Formation Rules:**
1. **Noun + Noun:** ile ijọsin (church = house + worship)
2. **Verb Nominalization:** i- + verb root = noun (ikẹkọ from kẹkọ)
3. **Reduplication:** pin → pinpin (share → sharing)
4. **Prefixation:** o-lu-kọ-ni (one-who-teaches = teacher/coach)

**Possessive Constructions:**
- **ti** marker: Ẹmi ti Ọlọrun (Spirit of God)
- **Compound:** ọmọ-ẹhin Jesu (disciple of Jesus)
- **Context:** awọn aṣẹ Jesu (commands of Jesus)

**Verb Forms:**
- **Base:** kọ (teach), gbọ (hear), pin (share)
- **Gerund:** kikọ (teaching), gbigbọ (hearing), pinpin (sharing)
- **Imperative:** Same as base form in many cases

## Translation Consistency Guidelines

**For Translators:**

1. **Maintain tone marks:** Use proper diacritics (á, à, ẹ, ọ, ṣ) for accuracy and meaning distinction
2. **Use standard Christian terms:** Follow Yoruba Bible (Bíbélì Mímọ́) translation conventions
3. **Preserve compound structures:** Keep hyphenation in compounds (ọmọ-ẹhin, not omo ehin)
4. **Apply consistent capitalization:** Capitalize deity terms (Ọlọrun, Jesu, Ẹmi Mimọ, Olúwa)
5. **Use indigenous vocabulary:** Prefer Yoruba roots over foreign borrowings when possible
6. **Distinguish vowels carefully:** Maintain e/ẹ and o/ọ distinctions (critical for meaning)
7. **Mark tones consistently:** Either use full tone marking or none; avoid partial marking
8. **Respect traditional terms:** Understand historical context of adapted pre-Christian vocabulary

**Common Pitfalls to Avoid:**
- Confusing ẹ with e, ọ with o (changes meaning)
- Omitting tone marks entirely (reduces clarity)
- Using òrìṣà terminology for Christian concepts (theological confusion)
- Inconsistent hyphenation in compounds
- Mixing orthographic conventions within same document
