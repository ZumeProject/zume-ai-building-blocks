## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus
- **Recommended Malay:** "Yesus" or "Isa Almasih"
- **Strategy Considerations:**
  - **"Yesus"** - Standard Malaysian Christian term (from Portuguese/Dutch influences)
  - **"Isa" or "Isa Almasih"** - Islamic/Arabic form used in Indonesian translation
  - **Decision Point:** Choose based on target audience:
    - Use "Yesus" for predominantly Christian Malaysian contexts
    - Use "Isa Almasih" for Muslim-friendly/interfaith contexts (following Indonesian model)
- **Recommended Examples:**
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands" → "Pelajari cara mematuhi perintah Yesus" (Christian) / "Pelajari cara mematuhi perintah Isa" (Islamic-friendly)
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "Cara Mengikut Yesus" / "Cara Mengikut Isa Almasih"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "Apakah pengikut Yesus?" / "Apakah pengikut Isa Almasih?"
  - "Becoming more like Jesus... Together" → "Menjadi lebih seperti Yesus... Bersama-sama"

### Christ
- **Recommended Malay:** "Kristus" or "Almasih" (Messiah)
- **Notes:**
  - "Kristus" is standard Christian term
  - "Almasih" is Islamic/Arabic term meaning "Messiah"
  - Often combined: "Yesus Kristus" or "Isa Almasih"
- **Pattern:** Typically appears as part of full title

### God
- **Recommended Malay:** "Allah"
- **Notes:**
  - "Allah" is the standard term for God in Malay (both Christian and Muslim contexts)
  - Universal usage across religious communities in Malaysia
  - Some controversy exists about Christian use of "Allah" in Malaysia, but it remains the standard Biblical translation term
- **Recommended Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Allah Menggunakan Orang Biasa"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Mendengar dan Mematuhi Allah"
  - "Sharing God's Story" → "Berkongsi Kisah Allah"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Menjadi murid bermakna kita mendengar daripada Allah dan kita mematuhi Allah"

### Holy Spirit
- **Recommended Malay:** "Roh Kudus" or "Roh Allah yang Kudus"
- **Notes:**
  - "Roh Kudus" is standard Christian usage
  - "Roh Allah yang Kudus" (Holy Spirit of God) follows Indonesian model
  - Malaysian Malay: "Roh Kudus"
  - Indonesian model: "Roh Suci Allah"
- **Recommended Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "Minta Roh Kudus Allah memimpin masa bersama anda"

### Spirit
- **Recommended Malay:** "Roh" (spirit - noun) or "Rohani" (spiritual - adjective)
- **Notes:** Identical to Indonesian usage
- **Recommended Examples:**
  - "Spiritual Breathing" → "Pernafasan Rohani"
  - "Spiritual Economy" → "Ekonomi Rohani"

### Lord
- **Recommended Malay:** "Tuhan" or "Allah"
- **Notes:**
  - "Tuhan" = Lord/Master (general term)
  - "Allah" can also mean Lord in religious contexts
  - Context-dependent choice
- **Recommended Examples:**
  - "The Lord's Supper" → "Perjamuan Tuhan"

## Key Translation Principles for Malay

### 1. Choose Your Contextual Strategy

**Option A: Standard Malaysian Christian Terminology**
- **Target:** Malaysian Christian community
- **Use:** "Yesus", "Gereja", "Baptisan"
- **Advantage:** Familiar to Malaysian Christians
- **Consideration:** May be less accessible to Muslim audiences

**Option B: Islamic-Friendly Terminology (Following Indonesian Model)**
- **Target:** Interfaith/Muslim-accessible contexts
- **Use:** "Isa Almasih", "Jemaah", "Pemandian"
- **Advantage:** Bridges Christian and Muslim contexts
- **Consideration:** May sound unfamiliar to Malaysian Christians

**Recommendation:** Choose Option A for Malaysian Christian contexts, Option B for broader interfaith ministry, or create two translation variants.

### 2. Malaysian vs Indonesian Malay Differences

Be aware of key spelling and vocabulary differences:

| Concept | Malaysian Malay | Indonesian |
|---------|-----------------|------------|
| Training | Latihan | Pelatihan |
| Leadership | Kepimpinan | Kepemimpinan |
| Multiplication | Penggandaan | Pelipatgandaan |
| Obey | Mematuhi | Menaati |
| Simple | Mudah | Sederhana |
| Network | Rangkaian | Jaringan |

### 3. Use of "Allah"

- **Standard Usage:** "Allah" is the standard term for God in Malay Bible translations
- **Context Awareness:** Note that in Malaysia there has been controversy about Christian use of "Allah"
- **Consistency:** Despite controversy, maintain consistency with established Bible translation practice
- **Alternative:** In very sensitive contexts, consider "Tuhan" (Lord/God) though this is less standard

### 4. Honorifics and Respect

Unlike Indonesian translation which uses "AS" (Alaihissalam) with "Isa Almasih AS", Malaysian Christian usage typically does not include Islamic honorifics with Jesus' name.

### 5. Consistency in Core Terms

Choose your strategy (Christian or Islamic-friendly) and maintain it consistently:
- **Murid** for disciple
- **Allah** for God
- **Injil** or **Berita Baik** for gospel
- **Gereja** or **Jemaah** for church (based on chosen strategy)

### 6. Practical Ministry Focus

Emphasize practical, accessible language:
- **Latihan** (training)
- **Penggandaan** (multiplication)
- **Kepimpinan** (leadership)
- Rather than overly theological terminology

## Comparison: Malay vs Indonesian Translation Strategies

### Similarities
Both languages share:
- Core vocabulary: Murid (disciple), Allah (God), Injil (gospel), Doa (prayer)
- Islamic-influenced religious terminology
- Austronesian grammar structures

### Key Differences

| Term | Indonesian Strategy | Recommended Malay Strategy |
|------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| Jesus | Isa (Islamic) | Yesus (Christian) or Isa (Islamic-friendly) |
| Church | Jemaah (Islamic term) | Gereja (Christian) or Jemaah (Islamic-friendly) |
| Baptism | Permandian (washing) | Baptisan (Christian) or Pemandian (Islamic-friendly) |
| Training | Pelatihan | Latihan |
| Multiplication | Pelipatgandaan | Penggandaan |
| Leadership | Kepemimpinan | Kepimpinan |
| Simple | Sederhana | Mudah |

### Strategic Consideration

The Indonesian translation takes a **strongly Islamic-friendly approach**, using "Isa" instead of "Yesus" and "Jemaah" instead of "Gereja".

For Malay translation, consider:
1. **Malaysian Christian context**: Use standard Christian terminology that Malaysian believers are familiar with
2. **Interfaith/outreach context**: Follow Indonesian model more closely
3. **Dual translation approach**: Create two variants for different contexts

## Appendix: Malaysian Malay Language Notes

### Spelling Conventions
- Use "k" not "q" in most loanwords (Alkitab not Alqitab)
- Use "c" for "ch" sound (gereja not geredja)
- Follow Malaysian standard spelling (EYD)

### Common Affixes
- **me-** prefix for verbs (melatih = to train)
- **-kan** suffix for causative (mematuhi vs patuhi)
- **-an** suffix for nouns (latihan, penggandaan)
- **pe-/-pem-/-per-** for agent nouns (pemimpin = leader)

### Sentence Structure
- Subject-Verb-Object (similar to English)
- No verb conjugation by tense (use time markers)
- Formal vs informal registers (use formal for religious content)
