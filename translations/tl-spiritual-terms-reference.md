**Purpose:** Reference guide for maintaining consistent spiritual terminology in Tagalog translations

## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Tagalog translations for use in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy navigates the complex linguistic landscape of Filipino Christianity, which reflects over 300 years of Spanish colonial influence blended with indigenous Tagalog spiritual concepts. This guide documents both Spanish loanwords and native Tagalog terms, providing translators with etymological background and cultural context to maintain consistency and cultural appropriateness.

**Note:** The current PO file (zume-tl.po) contains primarily empty translations (msgstr ""). This reference guide provides recommended translations based on established Filipino Christian terminology, biblical translation standards, and cultural-linguistic research.

---

## Historical and Linguistic Context

### Spanish Colonial Influence (1565-1898)
- **333 years of Spanish rule** established Catholic Christianity as dominant
- **20-33% of modern Tagalog vocabulary** derives from Spanish
- **Religious terminology heavily influenced** by Catholic liturgical Spanish
- Spanish friars strategically used **indigenous terms** (like "simbahan") when native concepts aligned with Christian ideas

### Protestant Influence (1898-Present)
- **American colonial period** introduced Protestant missionary work
- **English loanwords** entered evangelical vocabulary
- **Protestant Bible translations** established standardized spiritual terminology
- Modern translations balance **traditional Catholic terms** with **evangelical concepts**

### Indigenous Spiritual Heritage
- **Pre-colonial spiritual concepts** (bathala, anito, diwata) provided foundation
- **Native words for worship** (simba/samba) preceded Christianity
- **Reverence terminology** already existed before Spanish arrival
- Modern usage **retains indigenous roots** while adapting Christian meanings

## Core Deity Terms

### God
- **Tagalog:** "Diyos" or "Panginoon"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Diyos" - Spanish loanword from "Dios"
  - "Panginoon" - Native Tagalog word meaning "Lord/Master" (from root "ginoo" = lord, master)
- **Historical Note:** Pre-colonial supreme deity was "Bathala," but this term largely replaced by Spanish "Diyos"
- **Usage Contexts:**
  - "Diyos" - Most common, neutral reference to God
  - "Panginoon" - Emphasizes lordship, authority, reverence
  - "Ama sa Langit" - Heavenly Father (more intimate)
- **Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Ginagamit ng Diyos ang mga Ordinaryong Tao"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Pakikinig at Pagsunod sa Diyos"
  - "What do you learn about God?" → "Ano ang natutunan mo tungkol sa Diyos?"
  - "God's voice" → "tinig ng Diyos" or "boses ng Panginoon"

### Jesus / Jesus Christ
- **Tagalog:** "Hesus" or "Hesukristo" (Jesus Christ)
- **Etymology:**
  - Spanish loanword from "Jesús/Jesucristo"
  - Adapted to Tagalog phonology (J → H sound shift)
- **Formal Titles:**
  - "Hesukristo" - Jesus Christ (formal, theological)
  - "Panginoong Hesus" - Lord Jesus
  - "Tagapagligtas" - Savior (native Tagalog)
- **Usage Notes:**
  - Use "Hesus" in conversational/training contexts
  - Use "Hesukristo" in theological/doctrinal statements
  - Possessive: "ni Hesus" (of Jesus) or "kay Hesus" (to/with Jesus)
- **Examples:**
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "Paano Sumunod kay Hesus"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "Ano ang kahulugan ng tagasunod ni Hesus?"
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands" → "Matutong sumunod sa mga utos ni Hesus"
  - "Becoming more like Jesus" → "Pagiging katulad ni Hesus"

### Christ / Messiah
- **Tagalog:** "Cristo" (Christ), "Mesias" (Messiah)
- **Etymology:**
  - "Cristo" - Spanish from "Cristo"
  - "Mesias" - Spanish from "Mesías"
- **Usage Pattern:** Primarily appears in compound "Hesukristo" (Jesus Christ)
- **Examples:**
  - "Body of Christ" → "Katawan ni Cristo"
  - "in Christ" → "kay Cristo"

### Holy Spirit
- **Tagalog:** "Espiritu Santo" or "Banal na Espiritu"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Espiritu" - Spanish loanword from "Espíritu"
  - "Santo" - Spanish loanword from "Santo"
  - "Banal" - Native Tagalog word meaning "holy/sacred"
- **Variants:**
  - "Espiritu Santo" - Most common (fully Spanish-derived)
  - "Banal na Espiritu" - Mixed form (Tagalog + Spanish)
  - "Espiritu ng Diyos" - Spirit of God
- **Usage Notes:** "Espiritu Santo" more common in Catholic contexts; both acceptable
- **Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "Hilingin sa Espiritu Santo na pangunahan ang inyong pagsasama"
  - "filled with the Holy Spirit" → "napuspos ng Espiritu Santo"

### Spirit / Spiritual
- **Tagalog:**
  - "Espiritu" (spirit - noun, from Spanish)
  - "Ispirituwal" (spiritual - adjective, from Spanish "espiritual")
  - "Espiritwalidad" (spirituality - noun)
- **Native Alternative:** "Kaluluwa" (soul/spirit - native Tagalog)
- **Examples:**
  - "Spiritual Breathing" → "Ispirituwal na Paghinga"
  - "Spiritual Economy" → "Ispirituwal na Ekonomiya"
  - "spiritual growth" → "ispirituwal na paglaki"
  - "spiritual families" → "ispirituwal na pamilya"

### Lord
- **Tagalog:** "Panginoon"
- **Etymology:** Native Tagalog from "ginoo" (master/lord) with prefix "pang-" and suffix "-an"
- **Usage Contexts:**
  - Title for God or Jesus
  - Emphasis on authority and lordship
  - Used in liturgical/formal contexts
- **Examples:**
  - "The Lord's Supper" → "Hapunan ng Panginoon"
  - "Lord Jesus" → "Panginoong Hesus"
  - "The Lord's Prayer" → "Panalangin ng Panginoon"

### Father (God the Father)
- **Tagalog:** "Ama" or "Ama sa Langit" (Heavenly Father)
- **Etymology:**
  - "Ama" - Native Tagalog word for father
  - "Langit" - Native Tagalog word for heaven/sky
- **Modern Usage Distinction:**
  - "Ama" - More formal, religious (traditional Catholic/Protestant)
  - "Tatay" - Intimate, personal (modern translations like Pinoy Bible Version)
- **Examples:**
  - "Heavenly Father" → "Ama sa Langit"
  - "God the Father" → "Diyos Ama"
  - "Our Father" → "Aming Ama" (Lord's Prayer opening)

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
- **Tagalog:** "Alagad" or "Disipulo"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Alagad" - Native Tagalog word meaning follower, adherent, devotee
  - "Disipulo" - Spanish loanword from "discípulo"
- **Contextual Usage:**
  - "Alagad" - More common in biblical/traditional contexts
  - "Disipulo" - Used in educational/training contexts
  - Both acceptable and interchangeable
- **Plural Forms:** "mga alagad" / "mga disipulo"
- **Examples:**
  - "What's a Disciple?" → "Ano ang Alagad?" or "Ano ang Disipulo?"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and obey God" → "Ang pagiging alagad ay nangangahulugan ng pakikinig at pagsunod sa Diyos"
  - "multiplying disciples" → "mga alagad na lumalaki" or "mga disipulong nag-multiply"
  - "trained disciple" → "sinanay na alagad"

### Disciple-making / Discipleship
- **Tagalog:**
  - "Pag-aalagang pang-espirituwal" (spiritual formation)
  - "Paggawa ng mga alagad" (making disciples)
  - "Pagdidisipulo" (discipleship process)
- **Etymology:** Native Tagalog verbal prefix "pag-" + root word
- **Examples:**
  - "Duckling Discipleship" → "Pagdidisipulong Parang Bibi"
  - "Learn what ducklings have to do with disciple-making" → "Alamin kung ano ang kaugnayan ng mga bibi sa paggawa ng mga alagad"
  - "Train disciples how to make disciples" → "Sanayin ang mga alagad kung paano gumawa ng mga alagad"

### Follower
- **Tagalog:** "Tagasunod" or "Sumusunod"
- **Etymology:** Native Tagalog "sunod" (follow) with prefix "taga-" (one who does)
- **Related Terms:**
  - "Sumusunod" - Present tense (one who is following)
  - "Sumunod" - Verb (to follow)
  - "Pagsunod" - Noun (following/obedience)
- **Examples:**
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "Ano ang tagasunod ni Hesus?"
  - "follower of Christ" → "tagasunod ni Cristo"
  - "help others become followers" → "tumulong sa iba na maging mga tagasunod"

### Follow / Following
- **Tagalog:** "Sumunod" (verb), "Pagsunod" (noun), "Sumusunod" (gerund)
- **Etymology:** Native Tagalog root "sunod"
- **Examples:**
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "Paano Sumunod kay Hesus"
  - "following Jesus" → "pagsunod kay Hesus" or "sumusunod kay Hesus"

### Believer
- **Tagalog:** "Mananampalataya" or "Naniniwala"
- **Etymology:**
  - From native root "sampalataya/panatag" (faith/trust)
  - "Naniniwala" from root "paniwala" (belief)
- **Related Terms:**
  - "Hindi naniniwala" or "Di-mananampalataya" (unbeliever)
  - "Pananampalataya" (faith - noun)
- **Examples:**
  - "Share with a believer" → "Ibahagi sa mananampalataya"
  - "believers in Christ" → "mga naniniwala kay Cristo"

### Obey / Obedience
- **Tagalog:**
  - "Sumunod" or "Tumalima" (to obey - verb)
  - "Pagsunod" or "Pagsunod" (obedience - noun)
  - "Masunurin" (obedient - adjective)
- **Etymology:**
  - "Sunod" - Native Tagalog (follow)
  - "Talima" - Native Tagalog (comply)
- **Examples:**
  - "Spiritual Breathing is Hearing and Obeying God" → "Ang Ispirituwal na Paghinga ay Pakikinig at Pagsunod sa Diyos"

## Church Terms

### Church
- **Tagalog:** "Simbahan" or "Iglesia"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Simbahan" - From Malay "sembah" (worship) + Tagalog suffix "-an" (place of)
  - "Iglesia" - Spanish loanword from "iglesia" (ultimately from Greek "ekklesia")
- **Historical Context:**
  - Spanish friars used indigenous "simbahan" (worship place) instead of importing "iglesia"
  - Traditional distinction: "simbahan" = building; "iglesia" = congregation
  - Modern usage: Both can refer to building and congregation
- **Usage Guidelines:**
  - "Simbahan" - More common, indigenous feel, house church contexts
  - "Iglesia" - More formal, institutional contexts
  - For Zume materials: Recommend "simbahan" for simple church emphasis
- **Examples:**
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "Simpleng Kahulugan ng Alagad at Simbahan"
  - "When you think of a church, what comes to mind?" → "Kapag naisip mo ang simbahan, ano ang pumapasok sa isip mo?"
  - "What's a Church?" → "Ano ang Simbahan?"
  - "My Churches" → "Aking mga Simbahan"

### Simple Church
- **Tagalog:** "Simpleng Simbahan" or "Payak na Simbahan"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Simple" - English/Spanish loanword
  - "Payak" - Native Tagalog (simple, plain, humble)
- **Usage Notes:** "Payak" has connotations of humility and simplicity (positive values)
- **Examples:**
  - "2 simple churches per 5,000 people" → "2 simpleng simbahan bawat 5,000 tao"
  - "Always report new simple churches" → "Laging iulat ang mga bagong simpleng simbahan"
  - "Leading a Simple Church" → "Pamumuno sa Simpleng Simbahan"

### Congregation / Assembly
- **Tagalog:**
  - "Kapulungan" (assembly - native Tagalog)
  - "Kongregasyon" (congregation - Spanish/English loanword)
  - "Kapisanan" (fellowship/association - native)
- **Etymology:** Native roots "pulong" (meeting) and "kapisanan" (fellowship)

### Body of Christ
- **Tagalog:** "Katawan ni Cristo"
- **Etymology:** Native "katawan" (body) + possession marker "ni"
- **Examples:**
  - "Pray and thank God for the Body of Christ" → "Manalangin at magpasalamat sa Diyos para sa Katawan ni Cristo"

## Gospel and Scripture Terms

### Gospel
- **Tagalog:** "Ebanghelyo" or "Mabuting Balita"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Ebanghelyo" - Spanish loanword from "evangelio"
  - "Mabuting Balita" - Native Tagalog (literally "Good News")
- **Usage Contexts:**
  - "Ebanghelyo" - More theological, formal
  - "Mabuting Balita" - More accessible, literal translation
  - Both acceptable; "Mabuting Balita" may be more culturally accessible
- **Examples:**
  - "The Gospel and How to Share It" → "Ang Ebanghelyo at Paano Itong Ibahagi"
  - "3-Circles Gospel Presentation" → "3-Bilog na Presentasyon ng Ebanghelyo"
  - "Share the Gospel" → "Ibahagi ang Mabuting Balita"
  - "Practice Sharing the Gospel" → "Pagsasanay: Pagbabahagi ng Ebanghelyo"

### Good News
- **Tagalog:** "Mabuting Balita"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Mabuti" - Native Tagalog (good)
  - "Balita" - Native Tagalog (news)
- **Notes:** Direct translation; equivalent to "Gospel"

### Bible
- **Tagalog:** "Biblia" or "Banal na Kasulatan"
- **Etymology:**
  - "Biblia" - Spanish loanword from "Biblia"
  - "Banal na Kasulatan" - Native + Spanish hybrid (Holy Scriptures)
    - "Banal" - Native Tagalog (holy/sacred)
    - "Kasulatan" - Native Tagalog from "sulat" (writing)
- **Adjective Form:** "Biblikal" or "mula sa Biblia" (biblical)
- **Examples:**
  - "S.O.A.P.S. Bible Study" → "S.O.A.P.S. Pag-aaral ng Biblia"
  - "S.O.A.P.S. Bible Reading" → "S.O.A.P.S. Pagbasa ng Biblia"
  - "Bible Reading" → "Pagbasa ng Biblia"
  - "Personal Bible Study" → "Personal na Pag-aaral ng Biblia"

### Scripture(s)
- **Tagalog:** "Banal na Kasulatan" or "Kasulatan"
- **Etymology:** Native Tagalog "kasulatan" from root "sulat" (write)
- **Usage Notes:**
  - Singular and plural same form in Tagalog
  - "Banal" (holy) often added for reverence
- **Examples:**
  - "Scripture" → "Banal na Kasulatan"
  - "the Scriptures" → "ang mga Kasulatan"

### Word / Word of God
- **Tagalog:** "Salita" or "Salita ng Diyos"
- **Etymology:** Native Tagalog "salita" (word)
- **Examples:**
  - "Word of God" → "Salita ng Diyos"
  - "God's Word" → "Salita ng Panginoon"

## Linguistic Patterns and Translation Principles

### 1. Spanish Colonial Legacy
**333 years of Spanish rule** left profound impact on religious vocabulary:
- **20-33% of Tagalog** derives from Spanish
- **Core religious terms** predominantly Spanish loanwords
- **Catholic liturgical language** shaped spiritual vocabulary
- **Phonological adaptation** (e.g., Jesús → Hesus, bautismo → binyag)

**Spanish Loanwords in Spiritual Terminology:**
- Diyos, Hesus, Cristo, Espiritu, Santo
- Iglesia, Pastor, Apostol, Propeta
- Binyag, Sakripisyo, Resureksyon, Misyon
- Ebanghelyo, Biblia, Grasya

### 2. Indigenous Tagalog Roots
**Pre-colonial spiritual concepts** provided foundation:
- **Worship terminology** predates Christianity (simba/samba → simbahan)
- **Native ethical concepts** (tapat, sala, ligtas, pala)
- **Relationship terms** (ama, anak, kapatid used for God/family imagery)

**Native Tagalog Spiritual Terms:**
- Panginoon, Panalangin, Kasalanan, Kaligtasan
- Pananampalataya, Katapatan, Pagsisisi
- Simbahan, Langit, Kapayapaan, Pag-ibig
- Alagad, Tagasunod, Pagpapala

### 3. English Influence (Modern)
**American colonial period and globalization** introduced English terms:
- **Modern ministry concepts** often retain English (coach, network, leader)
- **Technical terms** use English (training, session, checklist)
- **Youth/contemporary contexts** mix Taglish (Tagalog-English)

**English Loanwords in Modern Ministry:**
- Coach, Coaching, Network, Leader
- Training, Session, Workshop
- Vision casting, Multiplication (sometimes)

### 4. Hybrid Formation
**Tagalog creates new terms** through affixation and compounding:
- **Verbal nouns:** pag- + root (pagbabasa, pagsasanay, pagdidisipulo)
- **Agent nouns:** taga- + root (tagasunod, tagapagligtas, tagapagsanay)
- **Quality nouns:** ka- + root + -an (katapatan, kapayapaan, kasalanan)

### 5. Catholic vs. Protestant Distinctions

**Catholic Terminology** (traditional):
- Banal na Kumunyon (Holy Communion)
- Eukaristiya (Eucharist)
- Santo/Santa (Saints)
- Misa (Mass)
- Binyag (Baptism - traditional form)

**Protestant/Evangelical Terminology** (modern):
- Hapunan ng Panginoon (Lord's Supper)
- Mabuting Balita (Good News - alternative to Ebanghelyo)
- Bawtismo (Baptism - closer to English pronunciation)
- English loanwords (coach, network, leader, training)

## Translation Strategy for Zume Materials

### Core Principles

#### 1. Accessibility Over Formality
- **Prioritize native Tagalog** where possible for cultural resonance
- **Use Spanish loanwords** where they're standard and widely understood
- **Keep English terms** where they've become standard in Filipino evangelical contexts
- **Avoid archaic religious language** that creates distance

#### 2. Simple Church Context
- **Use "Simbahan"** rather than "Iglesia" (more indigenous, less institutional)
- **Emphasize "Simpleng Simbahan"** or "Payak na Simbahan" (simple church)
- **Use accessible terms:** "Bisyon" (vision), "Pagsasanay" (training), "Lider" (leader)
- **Avoid hierarchical titles:** Prefer "lider" over "pastor," "gabay" over formal titles

#### 3. Indigenous Spiritual Resonance
- **Leverage native terms** where they carry spiritual weight:
  - "Panalangin" (prayer) - deeply rooted in culture
  - "Pagpapala" (blessing) - native concept of fortune/divine favor
  - "Katapatan" (faithfulness) - cultural value of loyalty
  - "Pag-ibig" (love) - highest form of love

#### 4. Practical Action-Oriented Language
- **Use verbal nouns** for active concepts:
  - "Pagsunod" (following/obeying)
  - "Pagbabahagi" (sharing)
  - "Pagsasanay" (training)
  - "Pagdidisipulo" (discipling)

#### 5. Culturally Appropriate Mixing
**Taglish (Tagalog-English mixing)** is natural for many Filipino evangelicals:
- **Keep English** for modern ministry terms (coach, network, session)
- **Use Tagalog** for spiritual/relational concepts (alagad, panalangin, pagpapala)
- **Maintain balance** - don't overuse English in contexts where Tagalog is strong

#### 6. Respect for Catholic Heritage
- **Acknowledge Catholic terminology** where appropriate (most Filipinos are Catholic)
- **Use shared terms** that bridge Catholic/Protestant divide (Diyos, Hesus, Biblia)
- **Avoid exclusive Protestant jargon** that alienates Catholic-background believers

## Special Considerations for Tagalog Translation

### 1. Respect Markers (Po/Opo)
Tagalog uses **respect markers** (po/opo/ho/oho) in polite speech:
- Use when addressing God in prayer: "Panginoon, salamat po"
- Use in teaching contexts with older audiences
- Can be omitted in peer-to-peer discipleship contexts
- Recommendation: Use moderately to maintain respect without being overly formal

### 2. Inclusive vs. Exclusive "We"
Tagalog distinguishes **inclusive** (includes listener) from **exclusive** (excludes listener) pronouns:
- **Tayo/Atin/Natin** - We (including you)
- **Kami/Amin/Namin** - We (not including you)
- **Important for community language:** Use "tayo" for church/group contexts
- Example: "Tayo ay mga alagad" (We are disciples - including you)

### 3. Verb Focus System
Tagalog has **complex verb focus system** that affects translation:
- **Actor focus:** Emphasis on who does action
- **Object focus:** Emphasis on what receives action
- **Locative focus:** Emphasis on where action happens
- **Benefactive focus:** Emphasis on for whom action is done
- Recommendation: Use natural Tagalog verb forms; don't force English word order

### 4. Ligature Usage
**Ligature particles** (na/ng/-ng) connect modifiers:
- "Banal na Espiritu" (Holy Spirit) - adjective + ligature + noun
- "Simpleng simbahan" (Simple church) - ligature fuses with adjective
- "Alagad ni Hesus" (Disciple of Jesus) - possession marker
- Ensure proper ligature usage for natural-sounding Tagalog

### 5. Register and Formality
**Choose appropriate register** for audience:
- **Formal/Literary:** Biblical translations, doctrinal statements
- **Standard:** General teaching, training materials
- **Conversational:** Peer mentoring, testimonies
- **Taglish (mixed):** Youth contexts, modern ministry
- Recommendation for Zume: **Standard to Conversational** register

### 6. Indigenous Metaphor Adaptation
**Filipino cultural metaphors** differ from Western:
- **Family imagery** highly resonant (ama, anak, kapatid, pamilya)
- **Agricultural metaphors** work well (ani, tanim, butil)
- **Bayanihan spirit** (community cooperation) - good for church planting concepts
- **Pakikisama** (fellowship/harmony) - important cultural value
- Adapt Western metaphors to Filipino cultural context where needed

## Cultural and Historical Notes

### The Filipino Spiritual Landscape

**Religious Composition:**
- **80-85% Catholic** (Spanish colonial legacy)
- **10-15% Protestant/Evangelical** (American colonial influence + modern movements)
- Small Muslim minority (Southern Philippines)
- Indigenous animist traditions persist in some areas

**Implications for Translation:**
- Most readers have **Catholic background** but may be in Protestant/evangelical contexts
- **Shared Catholic-Protestant vocabulary** bridges contexts (Diyos, Hesus, Biblia, Espiritu Santo)
- **Protestant-specific terms** may need brief explanation (Hapunan ng Panginoon vs. Banal na Kumunyon)
- **Indigenous spiritual heritage** provides native concepts that can be redeemed for Christian use

### Language Dynamics

**Tagalog/Filipino Language:**
- **Tagalog** = ethnic language of Manila and surrounding regions
- **Filipino** = national language based on Tagalog with borrowings from other Philippine languages
- Used interchangeably in most contexts
- **Highly multilingual society:** Many Filipinos speak multiple languages

**Translation Considerations:**
- **Taglish (Tagalog-English mixing)** natural for many, especially urban/educated
- **Regional variations** exist - Manila Tagalog vs. provincial varieties
- **Generational differences:** Youth more English-influenced
- **Class/education factors:** More English in educated/urban contexts

### Contextualization Insights

From research on Filipino contextualization (Melba Maggay, José de Mesa):
- **Filipinos think in concrete images** - use stories, parables, examples
- **Community/relational orientation** - emphasize group/family over individual
- **Bayanihan spirit** (community cooperation) - resonates with church planting
- **Pakikisama** (getting along, harmony) - important for discipleship relationships
- **Utang na loob** (debt of gratitude) - can illustrate grace, but use carefully
- **Hiya** (shame/honor) - significant cultural value affecting witness
- **Need for non-Hispanicized Christianity** - recover indigenous concepts

**Application to Zume:**
- Use **story-based teaching** (Filipino preference)
- Emphasize **family/community language** (pamilya, kapatid, ama)
- Highlight **cooperative discipleship** (bayanihan-style)
- Use **relationship-focused terminology** (pakikisama, pakikipagkapatid)
- Balance **indigenous and borrowed terms** appropriately
