## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus (Jésus)
**Translation:** Jésus (masculine, singular)

**Usage Notes:**
- Standard French transliteration from Greek Ἰησοῦς
- Always capitalized
- No article when used as proper name
- Possessive: "de Jésus" (of Jesus)

**Examples:**
1. EN: "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands by going AND staying."
   FR: "Apprenez à obéir aux commandements de Jésus en partant ET en restant."

2. EN: "How to Follow Jesus"
   FR: "Comment suivre Jésus"

3. EN: "What is a follower of Jesus?"
   FR: "Qu'est-ce qu'un disciple de Jésus ?"

4. EN: "What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?"
   FR: "Que fait un disciple de Jésus ?"

5. EN: "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you"
   FR: "Enseigner aux autres à obéir à Jésus et à le suivre comme vous"

6. EN: "Speaking to people YOU KNOW about Jesus"
   FR: "Parler de Jésus aux personnes que VOUS CONNAISSEZ"

7. EN: "Speaking to people YOU DON'T KNOW about Jesus"
   FR: "Parler de Jésus aux personnes que VOUS NE CONNAISSEZ PAS"

8. EN: "Becoming more like Jesus... Together"

**Grammar Notes:**
- "commandements de Jésus" (Jesus' commands) - uses "de" for possession
- "disciple de Jésus" (follower/disciple of Jesus)
- "parler de Jésus" (to speak about Jesus)
- "suivre Jésus" (to follow Jesus) - direct object, no preposition

**Translation:** Esprit Saint (masculine, singular) / l'Esprit Saint

**Usage Notes:**

- This corpus uses "Esprit Saint" exclusively
- Always capitalized
- Requires definite article in most contexts: "l'Esprit Saint"
- Sometimes abbreviated in older texts as "E.S." but not in this corpus

**Examples:**

**Theological Note:**
French Protestant and Catholic traditions both use "Esprit Saint," making it ecumenically acceptable.

### Follow / Following (Suivre)
**Translation:** Suivre (verb)

**Usage Notes:**
- Direct object verb (no preposition): "suivre Jésus" (to follow Jesus)
- Present participle: suivant
- Infinitive commonly used
- Less technical than "disciple" but complementary concept

**Examples:**
1. EN: "How to Follow Jesus"
   FR: "Comment suivre Jésus"

2. EN: "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you"
   FR: "Enseigner aux autres à obéir à Jésus et à le suivre comme vous"

**Grammar Notes:**
- "suivre" - irregular -re verb
- Conjugation: je suis, tu suis, il/elle suit, nous suivons, vous suivez, ils/elles suivent
- "suivre Jésus" - direct object, no preposition
- "le suivre" - with pronoun object

**Note on "suivre" vs. "suis":**
Be careful with conjugation: "je suis" means both "I am" (être) and "I follow" (suivre) - context determines meaning.

## Cultural and Linguistic Notes

### Romance Language Patterns

**Gender Agreement:**
French requires all adjectives, articles, and past participles to agree in gender and number with the nouns they modify. This affects spiritual terminology significantly:

Examples:
- "église nouvelle" (new church - feminine)
- "disciple formé" (trained disciple - masculine)
- "foi obéissante" (obedient faith - feminine)
- "croissance spirituelle" (spiritual growth - feminine)

**Prepositions with Spiritual Terms:**
Different verbs require different prepositions, unlike English:

- "obéir à Dieu" (to obey God - requires "à")
- "croire en Dieu" (to believe in God - requires "en")
- "prier Dieu" (to pray to God - no preposition, or "à Dieu")
- "suivre Jésus" (to follow Jesus - direct object, no preposition)
- "parler de Jésus" (to speak about Jesus - requires "de")

**Word Order:**
Adjectives typically follow nouns in French, opposite of English:

- "église simple" (simple church) - not "simple église"
- "disciple multiplicateur" (multiplying disciple)
- "croissance spirituelle" (spiritual growth)
- "formation biblique" (biblical training)

### Catholic vs. Protestant Terminology

This corpus reflects **evangelical Protestant** terminology choices:

**Ecumenical Terms (used by both):**
- Jésus (Jesus)
- Dieu (God)
- Bible (Bible)
- Église (Church)

### Formal vs. Informal Register (Tu/Vous)

**Tu vs. Vous:**
French has two forms of "you":
- **Tu** (informal, singular) - used with friends, family, children
- **Vous** (formal singular or any plural) - used with strangers, authorities, professional contexts, or any plural group

**Usage in this Corpus:**
The corpus predominantly uses **VOUS** forms, showing:

Examples:
- "Apprenez à obéir" (You [plural/formal] learn to obey)
- "Qu'apprenez-vous sur Dieu ?" (What do you [plural/formal] learn about God?)
- "Comment pouvons-nous vous aider ?" (How can we help you [formal/plural]?)
- "Votre témoignage" (Your [formal/plural] testimony)
- "Demandez-vous" (Ask yourselves)

**Implications:**
1. **Suitable for group settings** - "Vous" works for addressing multiple people
2. **Respectful tone** - Formal register shows respect to learners
3. **Professional/teaching context** - Appropriate for training materials
4. **Age-neutral** - Works for all age groups
5. **Cross-cultural safety** - Formal is safer than informal when relationship unclear

**When Tu Might Be Used:**
- Personal devotional materials
- Youth-specific materials
- One-on-one mentoring
- Very close community contexts
