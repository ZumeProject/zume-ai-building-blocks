# Somali Translation Reference Guide (so)

**Source File:** `po_files/zume-so.po`
**Purpose:** Reference guide for maintaining consistent spiritual terminology in Somali translations
**Date Analyzed:** 2026-01-16

---

## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Somali translations as used in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy uses **Christian terminology adapted for Somali-speaking audiences**, primarily targeting the Somali diaspora and Christian communities in Somalia/Somaliland. Somalia is a **99%+ Muslim country** where Christianity is extremely restricted, making this translation particularly sensitive. The approach balances:

1. **Arabic loanwords** (shared Islamic-Christian vocabulary from Arabic)
2. **Native Cushitic Somali terms** (indigenous vocabulary)
3. **Christian-specific terminology** (distinct from Islamic usage)

The translation demonstrates awareness of the **Muslim-majority context** while maintaining clearly Christian content for discipleship and multiplication movements.

## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus / Jesus Christ
- **Somali:** Ciise Masiix
- **Script:** Latin (Somali uses modified Latin alphabet since 1972)
- **Etymology:**
  - **Ciise** - From Arabic عيسى (Isa), the Quranic/Islamic name for Jesus
  - **Masiix** - From Arabic المسيح (al-Masih), meaning "Messiah/Christ"
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **Ciise** - the SAME form used in Islam (Quranic name). This is different from Arabic Christian translations which use يسوع (Yasu'). In Somalia's Muslim-majority context, "Ciise" is the only recognizable form.
- **Context:** While the name is shared with Islam, the addition of "Masiix" (Christ) and the context makes the Christian meaning clear
- **Examples:**
  - "Ciise Masiix wuxuu yidhi" - "Jesus Christ said"
  - "xerta Ciise Masiix" - "disciples of Jesus Christ"
  - "sida uu Ciise Masiix noloshaada u saameeyay" - "how Jesus Christ has impacted your life"
  - "ku mukhuursiiya magaca Aabbaha, Wiilka iyo Ruuxa Qudduuska ah" - "baptizing in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit"

### Christ / Messiah
- **Somali:** Masiix / Masiixa
- **Etymology:** From Arabic المسيح (al-Masih)
- **Grammatical Forms:**
  - Masiix (nominative/general)
  - Masiixa (definite/with article)
- **Notes:** Shared between Christian and Islamic usage, but context clarifies Christian theological meaning
- **Examples:**
  - "xerta Masiixa" - "disciples of Christ"
  - "Ciise Masiix" - "Jesus Christ"
  - "barakada raacidda Masiixa" - "the blessing of following Christ"

### God
- **Somali:** Eebbe / Rabbiga
- **Etymology:**
  - **Eebbe** - Native Cushitic Somali word for God (pre-Islamic)
  - **Rabbiga** - From Arabic الرب (ar-Rabb), meaning "the Lord"
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **Eebbe** (indigenous Somali) as PRIMARY term, NOT **Allah** (Arabic). This is significant:
  - Eebbe is the traditional Somali word for God/Supreme Being
  - Predates Islam in Somali culture
  - Creates subtle distinction from Islamic terminology
  - More neutral/accessible than Allah in Christian context
- **Alternation:** Both Eebbe and Rabbiga are used interchangeably, with Eebbe being more common
- **Examples:**
  - "Eebbe wuxuu adeegsadaa dad caadi ah" - "God uses ordinary people"
  - "Neefsashada ruuxiga ahu waa in Eebbe la addeeco" - "Spiritual breathing is hearing and obeying God"
  - "Maqal oo addeec Eebbe" - "Hearing and obeying God"
  - "Erayga Eebbe" - "the Word of God"
  - "qorshaha Eebbe" - "God's plan"

### Holy Spirit
- **Somali:** Ruuxa Qudduuska ah / Ruuxa Qudduuska ahu
- **Etymology:** From Arabic الروح القدس (ar-Ruh al-Quds)
- **Literal Meaning:** "The Holy Spirit" (ah/ahu = "who is" - grammatical particle)
- **Notes:**
  - Ruuxa (the Spirit) - from Arabic روح (ruh)
  - Qudduuska (holy/sacred) - from Arabic قدس (quds - holiness)
  - Always capitalized
  - Refers to Third Person of Trinity (Christian usage)
- **Examples:**
  - "Ruuxa Qudduuska ah" - "the Holy Spirit"
  - "Waydiiso Ruuxa Qudduuska ah inuu wakhtigga hago" - "Ask the Holy Spirit to guide your time"
  - "ku mukhuursiiya magaca Aabbaha, Wiilka iyo Ruuxa Qudduuska ah" - "baptizing in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit"

### Lord
- **Somali:** Rabbiga
- **Etymology:** From Arabic الرب (ar-Rabb) with Somali definite article
- **Grammatical Form:** Rabbiga = "the Lord" (definite)
- **Usage:** Used both as title for God and in compound terms
- **Examples:**
  - "Cashada Rabbiga" - "the Lord's Supper"
  - "Waydiiso Rabbiga" - "Ask the Lord"
  - "codka Rabbiga" - "the voice of the Lord"
  - "dabaal degi doonaan cashada Rabbiga" - "will celebrate the Lord's Supper"

### Father (God the Father)
- **Somali:** Aabbaha
- **Etymology:** Native Somali word for "father" with definite article
- **Literal Meaning:** "the Father"
- **Usage:** Part of Trinitarian formula
- **Examples:**
  - "magaca Aabbaha, Wiilka iyo Ruuxa Qudduuska ah" - "the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit"
  - "shaqada Aabbaha" - "the work of the Father"

### Son (of God)

- **Usage:** Part of Trinitarian formula
- **Examples:**

### Spirit / Spiritual
- **Somali:** ruuxi / ruuxiga
- **Etymology:** From Arabic روح (ruh - spirit)
- **Grammatical Forms:**
  - ruuxi (spirit - adjectival)
  - ruuxiga (spiritual/of the spirit)

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
- **Somali:** xer / xerta
- **Etymology:** Uncertain - possibly native Somali or adapted term
- **Grammatical Forms:**

  - xerta (the disciple/disciples - definite)

- **Notes:** Very frequent term (100+ contexts). Core concept in Zúme training.
- **Examples:**

  - "xerta Ciise Masiix" - "disciples of Jesus Christ"
  - "Xer inaad ahaato" - "to be a disciple"
  - "xerta Masiixa" - "Christ's disciples"
  - "xer samaynta" - "disciple-making"

### Disciple-making / Discipleship
- **Somali:**

### Follower
- **Somali:** raacid / raacidda
- **Etymology:** From Somali root meaning "to follow"
- **Examples:**

### Obey / Obedience
- **Somali:**
  - addeec (obey - verb)
  - addeeco (obeying - present continuous)
  - addeecid (obedience - noun)
  - addeecay (obeyed - past tense)

### Hearing / Listening
- **Somali:** maqal / maqasho
- **Forms:**
  - maqal (hear - verb)
  - maqasho (hearing - gerund)
  - maqalno (we hear)
- **Usage:** Always paired with obedience in discipleship context
- **Examples:**

  - "inaan Eebbe maqalno oo aan Eebbe adeecno" - "that we hear God and obey God"
  - "Inaad codka Rabbiga maqasho" - "To hear the voice of the Lord"

## Church Terms

### Church
- **Somali:** kaniisad / kaniisadda / kiniisada
- **Etymology:** From Arabic/Syriac كنيسة (kanisah) - the traditional Christian term for church
- **Grammatical Variations:**
  - kaniisad (church - general)
  - kaniisadda (the church - definite)
  - kiniisada (alternate spelling)
  - kaniisadood (churches - plural)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **kaniisad** - the traditional Christian Arabic/Syriac loanword, NOT a neutral term like "gathering" or "assembly"
- **Significance:** This clearly identifies Christian community, distinguishing from Islamic worship (masjid/mosque)
- **Examples:**
  - "Sharraxaad fudud oo ku saabsan Xerta iyo Kiniisadda" - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church"
  - "Xer iyo Kiniisad" - "Disciples and the Church"
  - "kaniisadaha fudud" - "simple churches"
  - "jiilalka kaniisadaha" - "church generations"

### Simple Church
- **Somali:** kaniisad fudud / kaniisadaha fudud
- **Literal Meaning:** "simple church" / "the simple churches"
- **Notes:** Core concept for house church/CPM model
- **Examples:**
  - "2 kaniisadood oo sahlan" - "2 simple churches"
  - "beeritaanka kaniisadda" - "church planting"

### Spiritual Family
- **Somali:** qoys ruuxi ah / qoyska ruuxiga ah
- **Literal Meaning:** "spiritual family" / "the spiritual family"
- **Components:**
  - qoys (family - native Somali)

  - ah (being/is)
- **Examples:**

  - "qoysas ruuxi ah abuurno" - "we create spiritual families"

## Gospel and Scripture Terms

### Gospel
- **Somali:** Injiilka
- **Etymology:** From Arabic الإنجيل (al-Injil) - the Gospel
- **Grammatical Form:** Injiilka = "the Gospel" (definite article)
- **IMPORTANT NOTE:** الإنجيل (Injil) is used in both Christian and Islamic contexts:
  - **Islamic usage:** Refers to the revelation given to Prophet Isa (Jesus)
  - **Christian usage:** Refers to the Good News of Jesus Christ and the four Gospels
  - **Context makes Christian meaning clear**
- **Examples:**
  - "Injiilka iyo sidaad dadka ula wadaagi lahayd" - "The Gospel and How to Share It"
  - "Markhaati furka Injiilka" - "Gospel testimony/witness"
  - "3 Goobo Soo Bandhigida Injiilka" - "3 Circles Gospel Presentation"
  - "Injiilka oo wax lagu wacdiyo" - "Preaching/sharing the Gospel"

### Bible / Scripture
- **Somali:** Baybalka / Kitaabka Baybalka / Kitaabka
- **Etymology:**
  - **Baybalka** - From English "Bible" (transliteration)
  - **Kitaabka** - From Arabic الكتاب (al-Kitab - "the Book")
- **Usage:**
  - Baybalka - "the Bible" (direct borrowing from English)
  - Kitaabka Baybalka - "the Book of the Bible" / "the Biblical Book"
  - Kitaabka - "the Scripture/Book" (when context is clear)
- **Notes:** Somali Christians use "Baybalka" rather than just "Kitaabka" to distinguish from Quran (which Muslims call "al-Kitab")
- **Examples:**
  - "S.O.A.P.S. Barashada Baybalka" - "S.O.A.P.S. Bible Study"
  - "Barashada Kitaabka Baybalka ah" - "Bible reading/study"
  - "akhriska Baybalka" - "Bible reading"
  - "Kitaabka Baybalku wuxuu inoo sheegayaa" - "The Bible tells us"

### Word / Word of God
- **Somali:** Erayga Eebbe
- **Literal Meaning:** "the Word of God"
- **Components:**
  - Erayga (the Word/word)
  - Eebbe (God)
- **Examples:**
  - "Erayga Eebbe" - "the Word of God"
  - "fahanto, addeecdo oo aad dadka u la wadaagto Erayga Eebbe" - "understand, obey and share with people the Word of God"

### Good News
- **Somali:** warka wanaagsan
- **Literal Meaning:** "the good news"
- **Components:**
  - warka (the news/message)
  - wanaagsan (good/blessed)
- **Usage:** Used alongside or interchangeably with Injiilka (Gospel)
- **Examples:**
  - "warka wanaagsan Ee Eebbe" - "the good news of God"

## Sacraments and Practices

### Baptism / Baptize
- **Somali:** baabtiis / babtiisey / mukhuursiiya
- **Etymology:**
  - **baabtiis** - Likely from Arabic/Syriac baptisma (not found in extracted data)
  - **mukhuursiiya** - Active verb form meaning "baptize" (from Arabic root)
- **Grammatical Forms:**
  - mukhuursiiya (baptize/baptizing - verb)
  - ku mukhuursiiya (baptize in/into)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Baptism terminology is highly sensitive in Muslim contexts
- **Context:** Clear Christian sacramental practice
- **Examples:**
  - "ku mukhuursiiya magaca Aabbaha, Wiilka iyo Ruuxa Qudduuska ah" - "baptizing in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit"
  - "Taga o oxer ka dhiga Ummahada dunida oo dhan, oo ku mukhuursiiya" - "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them"

### Prayer / Pray
- **Somali:**
  - ducaysi (prayer - noun)
  - ducee / duceeya (pray/prays - verb)
  - ducaystaan (they pray)
  - duco (prayer/supplication)
  - tukasho (prayer - alternate term)
- **Etymology:**
  - **duco/ducaysi** - From Arabic دعاء (du'a - supplication/prayer)
  - **tukasho** - Possibly native Somali or adapted term
- **IMPORTANT NOTE:** دعاء (du'a) is used by both Christians and Muslims for prayer/supplication (distinct from صلاة - salah, the ritual Islamic prayers)
- **Examples:**
  - "u wada ducaystaan" - "pray together"
  - "U duceeya" - "Pray for"
  - "Ducaysta oo Eebbe uga mahad celiya" - "Pray and thank God"
  - "Ducada Barakada" - "The BLESS Prayer"
  - "tukashu" - "prayer"

### Lord's Supper / Communion
- **Somali:** Cashada Rabbiga
- **Literal Meaning:** "the Supper of the Lord"
- **Components:**
  - Cashada (the supper/meal)
  - Rabbiga (of the Lord)
- **Notes:** Distinctly Christian terminology for Eucharist/Communion
- **Examples:**
  - "Cashada Rabbiga iyo sidaad u hoggaamin lahayd" - "The Lord's Supper and How To Lead It"
  - "dabaal degi doonaan cashada Rabbiga" - "will celebrate the Lord's Supper"
  - "Dabaal degga cashada Rabbiga" - "Celebrating the Lord's Supper"

### Testimony / Witness
- **Somali:** markhaati fur / markhaati furkaaga / Markhaatiyadayda
- **Etymology:** From Arabic شهادة (shahadah - testimony/witness)
- **Grammatical Forms:**
  - markhaati (witness/testimony)
  - markhaati fur (testimony - compound)
  - markhaati furkaaga (your testimony)
  - Markhaatiyadayda (My witnesses)
  - markhaati furayaashiisa (His witnesses)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** شهادة (shahadah) in Islamic contexts specifically refers to the Muslim declaration of faith. In Christian Somali, it means testimony/witness. Context makes the Christian meaning clear.
- **Examples:**
  - "3 Daqiiqo oo markhaati furkaaga ah" - "3-Minute Testimony"
  - "markhaati furkaaga dadka kale ula wadaagi" - "share your testimony with others"
  - "Markhaati furka Injiilka" - "Gospel testimony"
  - "waxaad noqonaysiin Markhaatiyadayda" - "you will be My witnesses"
  - "waxaynu markhaati ka nahay" - "we are witnesses of"

- **Notes:** Uses native Somali number/doubling vocabulary rather than Arabic mathematical terms
- **Examples:**

- **Grammatical Forms:**

### 1. Hybrid Terminology Strategy

The Somali translation employs a **sophisticated hybrid approach**:

**English Borrowings:**

- Some technical terms in untranslated contexts

### 2. Critical Distinction: Eebbe vs. Allah

**MOST SIGNIFICANT FEATURE:**
The translation uses **Eebbe** (indigenous Somali word for God) as the PRIMARY term, NOT **Allah** (Arabic).

**Why This Matters:**
1. **Eebbe** predates Islam in Somali culture (ancient Cushitic deity concept)
2. Creates **subtle but clear distinction** from Islamic terminology
3. **More neutral** and accessible for Christian usage
4. Avoids direct association with Islamic theology
5. **Rabbiga** (from Arabic ar-Rabb "the Lord") used as secondary term

**Strategic Implication:**
This is a **contextualization strategy for Muslim-majority Somalia** - using indigenous vocabulary to express Christian concepts while maintaining cultural authenticity.

### 3. Muslim-Majority Context Awareness

**Somalia's Religious Context:**
- **99%+ Muslim** population
- Christianity is **illegal/extremely restricted**
- Conversion from Islam is **punishable by death**
- **Al-Shabaab** militant control in many areas
- Christian terminology must be **recognizable but distinct**

**Translation Approach:**
1. Uses **shared Arabic religious vocabulary** (Ciise, Masiix, Injiilka) for recognition
2. Employs **indigenous Somali terms** (Eebbe, xer) for distinction
3. Includes **explicit Christian content** (Trinity, baptism, Lord's Supper)
4. **NOT a Muslim-friendly bridge translation** - clearly Christian
5. Designed for **Christian diaspora** and **underground believers**

### 4. Trinitarian Clarity

**Full Trinitarian Formula Maintained:**
- "magaca Aabbaha, Wiilka iyo Ruuxa Qudduuska ah"
- "the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit"

**Significance:**
- Explicitly Christian theology
- Distinguishes from Islamic monotheism (Tawhid)
- No attempt to soften or contextualize

### 5. Practical Discipleship Focus

**Emphasis on:**
- **Action verbs:** addeec (obey), maqal (hear), hoggaamin (lead)
- **Multiplication:** laban laabanto (multiply), tiro badan (many)
- **Training:** tababar (training - 100+ occurrences)
- **Obedience:** addeec (central concept)

**Less Emphasis on:**
- Systematic theology
- Abstract doctrinal terms
- Traditional clergy titles
- Initial conversion/salvation language

### 6. Simple Church Paradigm

**Vocabulary Reflects CPM/DMM Values:**
- kaniisad fudud (simple church)
- qoys ruuxi ah (spiritual family)
- xer samaynta (disciple-making)
- hoggaaminta (leadership - non-hierarchical)
- tababar (training - every-believer)
- laban laabanto (multiplication focus)

**Avoids:**
- Traditional institutional church vocabulary
- Clergy titles (pastor, elder, bishop)
- Complex ecclesiastical terminology

### 7. Linguistic Features

**Somali Grammatical Characteristics:**
- **Definite articles:** -ka, -ta, -da (the)
  - kaniisad → kaniisadda (church → the church)
  - qoys → qoyska (family → the family)
- **Possessive forms:** -kiisa, -kaaga, -keenna
  - markhaati fur → markhaati furkaaga (testimony → your testimony)
- **Verb conjugations:** Complex system
  - addeec (obey) → addeecay (obeyed) → addeeco (obeying)
- **Compound constructions:**
  - qoys ruuxi ah (spiritual family - literally "family spiritual being")
  - wax qabadka (activity - literally "thing doing")

### 8. Script and Orthography

**Latin Script (Since 1972):**
- Somali officially uses **modified Latin alphabet**
- Previous Arabic script (Wadaad writing) largely abandoned
- This makes Christian materials **more accessible** than Arabic script
- **Distinct visual identity** from Arabic Islamic texts

**Modified Letters:**
- Uses: a, b, c, d, dh, e, f, g, h, i, j, k, l, m, n, o, q, r, s, t, u, w, x, y
- Special combinations: kh, sh, dh
- **No confusion with Arabic script** used for Quran
