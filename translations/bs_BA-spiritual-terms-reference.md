## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus
**Bosnian:** Isus
**Transliteration:** EE-soos
**Script:** Latin

**Contextual Examples:**

1. **"How to Follow Jesus"**
   → **"Kako slijediti Isusa"**
   (Kako SLEE-yeh-dee-tee EE-soo-sa)

2. **"What is a follower of Jesus?"**
   → **"Šta znači biti Isusov sljedbenik?"**
   (Shta ZNA-chee BEE-tee EE-soo-sov SLYED-ben-eek)

3. **"What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?"**
   → **"Koje su aktivnosti Isusova sljedbenika?"**
   (KO-yeh soo ak-teev-NOS-tee EE-soo-so-va slyed-BEN-ee-ka)

4. **"Learn how to obey Jesus' commands by going AND staying."**
   → **"Naučite kako slušati Isusove naredbe tako što ćete otići i ostati."**

5. **"Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you"**
   → **"Učite druge da slušaju i slijede Isusa s vama"**

6. **"Speaking to people YOU KNOW about Jesus"**
   → **"Govoriti ljudima koje POZNAJETE o Isusu"**

7. **"Speaking to people YOU DON'T KNOW about Jesus"**
   → **"Govoriti ljudima KOJE NE POZNAJETE o Isusu"**

8. **"Becoming more like Jesus... Together"**
   → **"Postati više kao Isus... Zajedno"**

**Grammatical Forms:**
- Nominative: Isus (Jesus/subject)
- Genitive: Isusa, Isusov, Isusova, Isusove (of Jesus/possessive)
- Accusative: Isusa (Jesus/object)
- Locative: Isusu (about Jesus)

**Cultural Notes:**
- "Isus" is the standard Christian term for Jesus in Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian
- Uses the same form across Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant communities
- The name is immediately recognizable and distinguished from Islamic "Isa" (عيسى)
- Latin script usage signals Christian (not Islamic) context in Bosnia

- Duh (Spirit - nominative)

**Transliteration:** gos-po-DAR / gos-PO-deen

   → **"Gospodnja večera i kako je voditi"**

   → **"Imajte Gospodnju večeru"**

**Grammatical Forms:**
- Gospodar (Lord - nominative masculine)
- Gospodin (Lord/Sir - more formal)
- Gospodnji/Gospodnja/Gospodnje (Lord's - possessive adjective)

**Usage Patterns:**
- "Gospodnji" primarily appears in fixed phrase "Gospodnja večera" (Lord's Supper)

- Etymologically from "gospod" (lord/master) + "-ar" (agent suffix)
- Socialist period reduced religious usage, strengthened secular meaning
- "Gospodnja večera" is traditional/liturgical phrase preserved from older translations
- Modern evangelicals often prefer direct "Isus" over formal titles

### Linguistic Characteristics of Bosnian

#### 1. **Script and Orthography**

- Bosnian uses **ijekavian dialect** (as does Croatian)

- Examples:
  - vjernik (believer) - ijekavian

- This locates translation in Bosnia/Herzegovina or Croatia

#### 3. **Grammar Features**

**Case System:**
Bosnian retains full Slavic 7-case system:
1. Nominative (subject)
2. Genitive (possession, "of")
3. Dative (indirect object, "to")
4. Accusative (direct object)
5. Vocative (address)
6. Locative (location, "in/on/about")
7. Instrumental (means, "with/by")

**Examples from corpus:**
- Isus (nominative) → Isusa (genitive/accusative) → Isusu (dative/locative)
- Bog (nominative) → Boga (genitive/accusative) → Bogu (dative/locative)
- Crkva (nominative) → crkve (genitive) → crkvi (locative)

**Aspect System:**
Bosnian verbs come in pairs:
- **Imperfective**: ongoing/repeated action
- **Perfective**: completed action

Examples:
- slušati (imperfective) → poslušati (perfective) = to listen/obey
- moliti (imperfective) → pomoliti (perfective) = to pray
- krstiti (imperfective) → pokrstiti (perfective) = to baptize

**Gender Agreement:**
Nouns, adjectives, and past tense verbs must agree in gender:
- učenik (masculine) → učenica (feminine)
- vjernik (masculine) → vjernica (feminine)
- duhovan (spiritual-masculine) → duhovna (spiritual-feminine)
