# Turkish Translation Reference Guide (tr_TR)

**Source File:** `po_files/zume-tr_TR.po`
**Purpose:** Reference guide for maintaining consistent spiritual terminology in Turkish translations
**Date Analyzed:** 2026-01-17

---

## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Turkish translations as used in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy employs **formal Christian Turkish terminology** designed for Turkish-speaking Christian audiences in the unique Turkish context:

**Critical Context:**
- **Muslim-majority country:** Turkey is 99%+ Muslim (predominantly Sunni)
- **Secular state:** Constitutional secularism (laiklik) since 1923 under Atatürk
- **Small Christian minority:** Less than 0.2% Christian (mostly historic Orthodox, Armenian, and Assyrian communities)
- **Sensitive environment:** Christian evangelism and conversion remain socially contentious
- **Language reform legacy:** 1928 Latin alphabet adoption and deliberate purging of Arabic/Persian vocabulary

**Translation Philosophy:**
The Turkish translation uses **standard Christian vocabulary** including traditional terms borrowed from Greek (Mesih, kilise, vaftiz) and employs the name **İsa** (from Arabic عيسى) for Jesus. This approach balances:
- Respect for historic Turkish Christian communities
- Familiarity for Muslim-background audiences (using İsa, not Yeshua)
- Clear Christian identity through church terminology (kilise, not cemaat)
- Practical discipleship emphasis suitable for a restrictive environment

**Linguistic Features:**
- **Turkification movement:** Atatürk's language reforms replaced Ottoman Turkish (heavy Arabic/Persian) with native Turkic vocabulary
- **Diglossia effect:** Religious vocabulary still retains Arabic/Persian loanwords despite purification efforts
- **Secular vocabulary preference:** Modern Turkish favors native Turkic roots over Islamic loanwords where possible
- **Agglutinative structure:** Turkish adds suffixes to roots for grammatical modifications

## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus
- **Turkish:** İsa
- **Etymology:** From Arabic **عيسى (Isa)** - the Quranic/Islamic form of Jesus
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **İsa** NOT the Hebrew-derived **Yeşu/Yeshua**. This choice reflects:
  - Familiarity to 99% Muslim Turkish population
  - Historic Turkish Christian community usage
  - Bridge term recognizable from Quranic tradition
  - Avoids foreign-sounding Hebrew forms
- **Cultural Context:** İsa is known to all Turks as a prophet (peygamber) in Islam, but translation clearly presents Him as Lord and Savior in Christian sense
- **Examples:**
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "İsa Nasıl Takip Edilir"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "İsa'nın bir takipçisi olmak nedir?"
  - "What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?" → "İsa'yı takip edenlerin faaliyetleri nelerdir?"
  - "Becoming more like Jesus... Together" → "Birlikte … daha çok İsa gibi olmak"
  - "Speaking to people YOU KNOW about Jesus" → "İsa hakkında tanıdığınız insanlarla konuşmak"
  - "Speaking to people YOU DON'T KNOW about Jesus" → "İsa hakkında henüz tanımadığınız insanlarla konuşmak"
  - "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you" → "Başkalarına sizinle birlikte İsa'ya itaat etmeyi ve O'nu takip etmeyi öğretmek" (implied)

### Christ / Messiah
- **Turkish:** Mesih
- **Etymology:** From Arabic **المسيح (al-Masih)** via Ottoman Turkish
- **Notes:** Mesih is shared between Christian and Islamic usage, but context clearly identifies Christian theological meaning
- **Usage:** Less frequent as standalone; İsa is preferred term
- **Related Terms:**
  - İsa Mesih (Jesus Christ/Jesus the Messiah) - when full title needed
  - Mesih'in bedeni (Body of Christ) - theological usage

### God
- **Turkish:** Tanrı
- **Etymology:** Native Turkic word **tengri/tanrı** (ancient Turkic deity concept)
- **CRITICAL CHOICE:** Uses **Tanrı** NOT **Allah**
  - **Tanrı** = Turkish nationalist/secularist preference, pre-Islamic Turkic heritage
  - **Allah** = Arabic loanword, Islamic connotation
- **Historical Context:**
  - Ottoman-era Turkish Christians used Allah (still used by Arabic Christians)
  - Republican-era Turkish (post-1923) promoted Tanrı as part of Turkification
  - Modern Turkish Bible translations typically use Tanrı to distinguish from Islamic Allah
  - Choice reflects secular Turkish identity and linguistic nationalism
- **Secular State Connection:** Using Tanrı aligns with Turkish Republic's secular ideology
- **Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Tanrı Sıradan İnsanları Kullanır"
  - "Spiritual Breathing is Hearing and Obeying God" → "Ruhsal Nefes Tanrı'yı Duymak ve İtaat Etmek Demektir"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Öğrenci olmak, Tanrı'dan duymak ve Tanrı'ya itaat etmek demektir"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Tanrı'yı Duymak ve O'na İtaat Etmek"
  - "Sharing God's Story" → "Tanrı'nın Hikayesini Paylaşmak"
  - "What do you learn about God?" → "Tanrı hakkında ne öğrendiniz?"
  - "Why is it essential to learn to hear and recognize God's voice?" → "Tanrı'nın sesini duymak ve tanımak neden çok önemlidir?"
  - "God makes plans, and He expects us to make plans, too" → "Tanrı planlar yapar ve bizden de planlar yapmamızı bekler"

### Holy Spirit
- **Turkish:** Kutsal Ruh / Mukaddes Ruh
- **Components:**
  - **Kutsal** = Holy (native Turkic: kut = sacred/blessed + -sal suffix)
  - **Mukaddes** = Holy (from Arabic مقدس muqaddas) - Ottoman-era term
  - **Ruh** = Spirit (from Arabic روح ruh)
- **Usage:** Both forms acceptable; Kutsal Ruh preferred in modern Turkish
- **Notes:**
  - Kutsal Ruh = Turkified, modern, secular-era preference
  - Mukaddes Ruh = Ottoman-era, more religious register
  - Always capitalized
  - Refers to Third Person of Trinity (distinct from Islamic understanding of ruh)
- **Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "Tanrı'nın Kutsal Ruhu'ndan zamanınıza önderlik etmesini isteyin" (implied)
  - "The Holy Spirit guides us" → "Kutsal Ruh bize rehberlik eder" (implied)
  - Trinitarian references using Baba (Father), Oğul (Son), Kutsal Ruh (Holy Spirit)

### Spirit / Spiritual
- **Turkish:**
  - **Ruh** = Spirit (noun) - from Arabic روح
  - **Ruhsal** = Spiritual (adjective) - ruh + -sal suffix
  - **Ruhani** = Spiritual (more religious register) - from Arabic/Persian روحانی
- **Usage Patterns:**
  - Ruhsal = Modern, secular-friendly, preferred in contemporary contexts
  - Ruhani = Traditional, religious, Ottoman-era feel
  - Translation consistently uses **ruhsal** for modern accessibility
- **Examples:**
  - "Spiritual Breathing" → "Ruhsal Nefes"
  - "Spiritual Economy" → "Ruhsal Ekonomi"
  - "The Spiritual Economy" → "Ruhsal İşleyiş"
  - "Keys to Spiritual Growth" → "Ruhsal Büyümenin Anahtarları"
  - "Spiritual Practices" → "Ruhsal Uygulamalar"
  - "spiritual families" → "ruhsal aileler" (implied)

### Lord
- **Turkish:** Rab
- **Etymology:** From Arabic **رب (Rabb)** - Lord/Master
- **Ottoman Legacy:** Retained despite Turkification due to religious context
- **Usage:** Primarily in compound terms and formal religious contexts
- **Examples:**
  - "The Lord's Supper" → "Rab'bin Sofrası"
  - "The Lord's Supper and How To Lead It" → "Rab'bin Sofrası Nedir ve Nasıl Yönetilir"
  - "Celebrate the Lord's Supper" → "Rab'bin Sofrasını Kutlayın"
  - "Lord Jesus" → "Rab İsa" (implied usage)

### Father (God the Father)
- **Turkish:** Baba / Ata
- **Etymology:**
  - **Baba** = Father (colloquial Turkish, more personal)
  - **Ata** = Father/Ancestor (formal Turkish, more reverential)
- **Usage:** Baba preferred for intimacy, Ata for formality
- **Trinitarian Context:** Baba, Oğul (Son), Kutsal Ruh (Holy Spirit)
- **Related:**
  - Göksel Baba / Semavi Ata (Heavenly Father)
  - Bizim Babamız (Our Father - in Lord's Prayer)

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
- **Turkish:** Öğrenci
- **Etymology:** From **öğrenmek** (to learn) + -ci suffix (one who does)
- **Literal Meaning:** Learner, student, pupil
- **CRITICAL DISTINCTION:**
  - **Öğrenci** = Student/learner (secular, educational context)
  - **Şagird** = Disciple (from Persian شاگرد) - more religious, master-apprentice connotation
  - Translation chose **öğrenci** for modern, accessible feel
- **Cultural Adaptation:** Avoids overtly religious Persian term (şagird), uses familiar educational vocabulary
- **Frequency:** Extremely common term (150+ contexts)
- **Examples:**
  - "Disciple" → "Öğrenci"
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "Öğrencinin ve Kilisenin Basit Tanımı"
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple & Church" → "Öğrenci & Kilise'nin Temel Tanımı"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Öğrenci olmak, Tanrı'dan duymak ve Tanrı'ya itaat etmek demektir"
  - "What's a Disciple?" → "Öğrenci Nedir?"
  - "Being A Disciple" → "Öğrenci Olmak"
  - "Disciples and the Church" → "Öğrenciler ve Kilise"
  - "Ask yourself, what does my disciple need" → "Kendinize şöyle sorun; öğrencimin neye ihtiyacı var?"
  - "1 trained multiplying disciple per 50,000 globally" → "dünya çapında her 50.000 kişi için 1 eğitimli çoğaltan öğrenci"

### Disciple-making / Discipleship
- **Turkish:**
  - **Öğrenci yetiştirmek** = Making disciples (literally: raising/training students)
  - **Öğrencilik** = Discipleship (state of being a disciple)
  - **Öğrencilik kavramları** = Discipleship concepts
- **Components:**
  - Yetiştirmek = to raise, train, cultivate (agricultural/parental imagery)
  - -lik suffix = creates abstract noun (state/quality of being)
- **Examples:**
  - "Making Disciples" → "Öğrenciler Yetiştirmek"
  - "Duckling Discipleship" → "Ördek Yavrusu Gibi Öğrenci Yetiştirmek"
  - "Duckling Discipleship – Leading Immediately" → "Ördek Yavrusu Gibi Öğrenci Yetiştirmek - Hemen Liderlik Yapmak"
  - "Discipleship Concepts" → "Öğrencilik Kavramları"
  - "To saturate the world with multiplying disciples in our generation" → "Bizim kuşağımızda çoğalan öğrencilerle dünyayı donatmak"
  - "Training Cycle for Maturing Disciples" → "Olgunlaşan Öğrencilerin Eğitim Aşamaları"
  - "Empowering Disciples. Anytime... Anywhere with the Zúme App" → "Öğrencileri Güçlendirmek. Her zaman… Zúme Uygulaması ile Her Yerde"
  - "Train disciples how to make disciples" → "Öğrencilere nasıl öğrenci yetiştirileceğini öğretin" (implied)

### Follower
- **Turkish:** Takipçi
- **Etymology:** From **takip etmek** (to follow) + -çi suffix (one who does)
- **Modern Feel:** Takipçi is contemporary Turkish (think social media "follower")
- **Accessibility:** More natural than religious alternative "mürit" (from Arabic)
- **Examples:**
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "İsa'nın bir takipçisi olmak nedir?"
  - "What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?" → "İsa'yı takip edenlerin faaliyetleri nelerdir?"
  - "follower of Jesus" → "İsa'nın takipçisi" / "İsa'yı takip edenler"

### Follow / Following
- **Turkish:** Takip etmek / İzlemek
- **Etymology:**
  - **Takip** = from Arabic تعقيب (ta'qib) - to follow/pursue
  - **İzlemek** = native Turkic - to follow/track
- **Usage:** Both acceptable; takip etmek more common in modern Turkish
- **Examples:**
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "İsa Nasıl Takip Edilir"
  - "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you" → "Başkalarına sizinle birlikte İsa'ya itaat etmeyi ve O'nu takip etmeyi öğretmek" (implied)

### Believer
- **Turkish:** İnanan / Mümin
- **Etymology:**
  - **İnanan** = One who believes (from inanmak - to believe + -an suffix)
  - **Mümin** = Believer (from Arabic مؤمن mu'min)
- **Choice:** Translation uses **İnanan** (native Turkish derivation)
- **Rationale:** Mümin has strong Islamic connotation in Turkish context
- **Contrast:**
  - "Believer" → "İnanan"
  - "Unbeliever" → "İmanlı Olmayan" (one without faith)
- **Note:** İmanlı Olmayan is interesting - uses iman (faith, from Arabic) but Turkish negative construction

### Believe / Faith
- **Turkish:**
  - **İnanmak** = To believe (verb) - native Turkic root
  - **İnanç** = Belief (noun) - from inanmak + -ç suffix
  - **İman** = Faith (noun) - from Arabic إيمان (iman)
- **Usage Pattern:**
  - İnanmak/inanç = general belief, secular-friendly
  - İman = religious faith, retained due to established usage
- **Cultural Note:** İman retained despite Arabic origin because no adequate Turkic substitute conveys religious faith concept
- **Examples:**
  - "The Power of Obedient Faith" → "İtaatkar İmanın Gücü"
  - "By faith..." → "İmanla..." (implied)

### Obey / Obedience
- **Turkish:** İtaat etmek / İtaat
- **Etymology:** From Arabic **طاعة (ta'ah)** - obedience
- **Retention Reason:** No native Turkic equivalent carries same religious authority concept
- **Forms:**
  - İtaat = obedience (noun)
  - İtaat etmek = to obey (verb)
  - İtaatkar = obedient (adjective)
- **Examples:**
  - "OBEY" → "İTAAT EDİN" (imperative, capitalized for emphasis)
  - "Obey" → "İtaat"
  - "Obeyed" → "İtaat Ettim" (past tense, first person)
  - "Spiritual Breathing is Hearing and Obeying God" → "Ruhsal Nefes Tanrı'yı Duymak ve İtaat Etmek Demektir"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Öğrenci olmak, Tanrı'dan duymak ve Tanrı'ya itaat etmek demektir"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Tanrı'yı Duymak ve O'na İtaat Etmek"
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands by going AND staying" → (English in source, Turkish would use İsa'nın emirlerine itaat etmek)
  - "The Power of Obedient Faith" → "İtaatkar İmanın Gücü"
  - "Heard, Obeyed, Shared, Trained" → "Duydum, İtaat Ettim, Paylaştım, Eğittim" (implied)

## Church Terms

### Church
- **Turkish:** Kilise
- **Etymology:** From Greek **ἐκκλησία (ekklesia)** → Old Church Slavonic/Russian церковь (tserkov) → Turkish kilise
- **Route:** Borrowed through Orthodox Christian Slavic influence, not directly from Greek
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **kilise** NOT **cemaat** (assembly/congregation)
  - **Kilise** = Church building and institution (Christian-specific term)
  - **Cemaat** = Congregation/community (used in Islamic context for mosque congregation)
- **Implications:**
  - Clear Christian identity, not Muslim-contextualized
  - Historic term familiar to Turks (visible church buildings in major cities)
  - Some negative connotations in conservative Muslim contexts
- **Secular Context:** Turkish Republic tolerates historic kilise buildings but restricts new church construction
- **Examples:**
  - "Church" → "Kilise"
  - "Churches" → "Kilise" (context-dependent, can be plural)
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "Öğrencinin ve Kilisenin Basit Tanımı"
  - "What's a Church?" → "Kilise Nedir?"
  - "When you think of a church, what comes to mind?" → "Bir kilise düşündüğünüzde aklınıza ne geliyor?"
  - "Disciples and the Church" → "Öğrenciler ve Kilise"
  - "My Churches" → "Kiliselerim"
  - "My Churches are Locked" → "Kiliselerim Kilitli"
  - "Church Name" → "Kilise adı"
  - "Church Location" → "Kilise Lokasyonu"
  - "Parent Church" → "Ana Kilise"
  - "Churches Needed" → "Kilise adı" (interesting - same as "Church Name")
  - "Churches Reported" → "Bildirilen Kiliseler"

### Simple Church
- **Turkish:** Basit kilise / Ev kilisesi
- **Components:**
  - **Basit** = Simple (from Arabic بسيط basit)
  - **Ev** = House/home (native Turkic)
  - **Ev kilisesi** = House church (alternative expression)
- **Cultural Sensitivity:** House church concept crucial in Turkey's restrictive environment
- **Legal Context:** Public church buildings face restrictions; house churches operate quietly
- **Examples:**
  - "Simple church" → "Basit kilise"
  - "Leading a Simple Church" → "Basit Bir Kiliseye Önderlik Etmek"
  - "Simple church vision map" → "Basit kilise vizyon haritası"
  - "Always report new simple churches that you are starting" → "Başladığınız yeni basit kiliseleri her zaman bildirin"
  - "2 Simple Churches" → "2 Basit Kilise"
  - "2 simple churches per 50,000 people globally" → "dünya genelinde 50.000 kişi başına 2 basit kilise"
  - "Map of Zúme Churches" → "2 Basit Kilise" (interesting mapping choice)
  - "Always Part of Two Churches" → "Her Zaman İki Kilisenin Bir Parçası"

### Body (of Christ)
- **Turkish:** Beden / Vücut
- **Etymology:**
  - **Beden** = Body (from Persian بدن badan)
  - **Vücut** = Body (from Arabic وجود wujud - originally "existence")
- **Usage:**
  - Beden = more common for physical body
  - Vücut = physical body, also used metaphorically
- **Examples:**
  - "Body Life" → "Vücut Yaşamı"
  - "Body of Christ" → "Mesih'in Bedeni" (implied)

### Congregation / Assembly
- **Turkish:** Cemaat / Topluluk
- **Notes:**
  - **Cemaat** (from Arabic جماعة jama'ah) = congregation/community (Islamic connotation in Turkish)
  - **Topluluk** = assembly/community (native Turkish, secular)
  - Translation avoids cemaat to maintain distinction from Islamic usage
  - Uses kilise (church) as primary term encompassing gathered believers

## Gospel and Scripture Terms

### Gospel
- **Turkish:** Müjde / İncil
- **Etymology:**
  - **Müjde** = Good news/glad tidings (from Persian مژده müzhde)
  - **İncil** = Gospel/New Testament (from Arabic إنجيل injil, ultimately from Greek εὐαγγέλιον evangelion)
- **IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:**
  - **Müjde** = The message/good news (abstract concept)
  - **İncil** = The Gospel book/New Testament (physical text)
- **Islamic Connection:** İncil recognized in Quran as scripture given to İsa (Jesus), but Muslims believe it was corrupted
- **Usage in Translation:**
  - Müjde preferred for "the Gospel message/good news"
  - İncil for "the Gospels" (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John)
- **Examples:**
  - "The Gospel" → "Müjde"
  - "The Gospel and How to Share It" → "Müjde Nedir ve Nasıl Paylaşılır"
  - "Share the Gospel" → "Müjde'yi Paylaşmak"
  - "Sharing the Gospel" → "Müjde'yi Paylaşmak"
  - "Practice Sharing the Gospel" → "Müjde'yi Paylaşmayı Pratik Edin"
  - "3 Circles Gospel Presentation" → "3 Çember Müjde Sunumu"
  - "3-Circles Gospel Presentation" → "3-Döngülü Müjde Sunumu"
  - "Take turns telling the Gospel to each other" → "Sırayla birbirinize Müjde'yi anlatın"

### Good News
- **Turkish:** Müjde / İyi haber
- **Components:**
  - **Müjde** = glad tidings (Persian loanword, established in Turkish)
  - **İyi haber** = literal "good news" (iyi = good, haber = news)
- **Usage:** Müjde preferred as established religious term
- **Examples:**
  - "Good News" → "Müjde" (implied from Gospel contexts)
  - "Sharing the good news" → "Müjde'yi paylaşmak" (implied)

### Bible
- **Turkish:** Kutsal Kitap / İncil
- **Components:**
  - **Kutsal Kitap** = Holy Book (kutsal = holy, kitap = book from Arabic كتاب)
  - **İncil** = Gospel/New Testament (can refer to entire Bible in some contexts)
- **Usage:**
  - Kutsal Kitap = entire Bible (Old + New Testament)
  - İncil = New Testament, or specifically the four Gospels
- **Ottoman Legacy:** Kitap (book) retained from Arabic despite Turkification
- **Examples:**
  - "Bible Reading" → "Kutsal Kitap Okuması"
  - "Personal Bible Study" → "Kişisel Kutsal Kitap Çalışması"
  - "S.O.A.P.S. Bible Study" → "K.İ.T.A.P. Yöntemi"
  - "S.O.A.P.S. Bible Reading" → "K.İ.T.A.P. Yöntemi"
  - "The Bible tells us..." → "Kutsal Kitap bize söyler..." (implied)

### Scripture
- **Turkish:** Ayet / Kutsal Yazılar
- **Etymology:**
  - **Ayet** = Verse/sign (from Arabic آية ayah)
  - **Kutsal Yazılar** = Holy Writings (kutsal = holy, yazılar = writings)
- **INTERESTING CHOICE:** Uses **Ayet** in S.O.A.P.S. method
- **Islamic Connection:** Ayet is the standard term for Quranic verses in Turkish
- **Implication:** Borrowing familiar Quranic terminology for Christian scripture
- **Examples:**
  - "Scripture" → "AYET" (in S.O.A.P.S. framework - capitalized)
  - "Holy Scriptures" → "Kutsal Yazılar" (implied)

### Word of God
- **Turkish:** Tanrı'nın Sözü / Tanrı'nın Kelâmı
- **Components:**
  - **Söz** = Word (native Turkic)
  - **Kelâm** = Word/speech (from Arabic كلام kalam - theological term)
  - **Tanrı'nın** = of God (genitive construction)
- **Preference:** Söz likely preferred for modern, accessible feel
- **Examples:**
  - "God's Word" → "Tanrı'nın Sözü" (implied)

## Sacraments and Practices

### Baptism / Baptize
- **Turkish:** Vaftiz
- **Etymology:** From Greek **βάπτισμα (baptisma)** → Slavic → Turkish vaftiz
- **Route:** Like kilise, came through Orthodox Christian Slavic influence
- **CRITICAL SENSITIVITY:** Baptism is THE most controversial Christian practice in Muslim Turkey
  - Conversion from Islam to Christianity (apostasy) remains deeply taboo
  - Public baptism can endanger converts
  - Many Turkish Christians baptized secretly
- **Forms:**
  - Vaftiz = baptism (noun)
  - Vaftiz etmek = to baptize (verb)
  - Vaftiz olmak = to be baptized (passive)
- **Examples:**
  - "Baptism" → "Vaftiz"
  - "Baptism and How To Do It" → "Vaftiz Nedir ve Nasıl Yapılır"
  - "Have you ever baptized someone?" → "Birisini vaftiz ettiniz mi?"
  - "IMPORTANT REMINDER – Have you been baptized?" → "ÖNEMLİ HATIRLATMA - Vaftiz oldunuz mu?"
  - "Baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit" → "Onları Baba, Oğul ve Kutsal Ruh adına vaftiz edin" (implied)

### Prayer / Pray
- **Turkish:** Dua / Dua etmek
- **Etymology:** From Arabic **دعاء (du'a)** - supplication/invocation
- **Universal Usage:** Used by both Muslims and Christians in Turkish
- **Distinction:** Context and content distinguish Christian prayer from Islamic salat
- **Forms:**
  - Dua = prayer (noun)
  - Dua etmek = to pray (verb)
  - Dua edin = pray! (imperative)
- **Examples:**
  - "Prayer" → "DUA" (capitalized in framework contexts)
  - "PRAY" → "DUA EDİN" (imperative, capitalized)
  - "How to Spend an Hour in Prayer" → "Dua Halkası - Dua Ederek Bir Saat Nasıl Geçirilir"
  - "Prayer Cycle" → "Dua Halkası"
  - "The Prayer Cycle" → "Dua Halkası"
  - "Prayer Walking" → "Dua Yürüyüşü"
  - "Prayer Walking and How To Do It" → "Dua Yürüyüşü Nedir ve Nasıl Yapılır"
  - "Prayer Walking Activity" → "Dua Yürüyüşü Etkinliği"
  - "Strong Prayer Life" → "Güçlü Dua Yaşamı"
  - "What is your reaction to spending an hour in prayer?" → "Bir saat boyunca dua etmekteki tepkin nedir?"
  - "Pray the Prayer Cycle for an hour individually" → "Bir saat süre ile bireysel olarak Dua Halkası'yla dua edin"
  - "I will Prayer Walk once every [days / weeks / months]" → "Dua Yürüyüşü'nü her [gün / hafta / ay] en az bir kez yapacağım"
  - "I will use the Prayer Cycle tool once every [days / weeks / months]" → "Dua Halkası aracını her [gün / hafta / ay] en az bir kez kullanacağım"

### Prayer Cycle
- **Turkish:** Dua Halkası
- **Components:**
  - **Dua** = Prayer
  - **Halkası** = Circle/ring/cycle (halka = circle + -sı possessive suffix)
- **Literal:** Circle of prayer / Prayer ring
- **Creative Translation:** Captures cyclic nature of prayer practice
- **Examples:** See prayer examples above

### BLESS Prayer
- **Turkish:** B.E.R.E.K.E.T. Duası
