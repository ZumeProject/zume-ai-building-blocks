## Core Deity Terms

### God
**Mongolian**: Бурхан (Burkhan)
**Transliteration**: Burkhan
**Literal Meaning**: Divine being, deity
**Source**: Borrowed from Tibetan Buddhism, originally meaning "Buddha"

#### Examples in Context:
1. **"God Uses Ordinary People"**
   - Бурхан жирийн хүмүүсийг ашигладаг
   - *Burkhan jiriin khumuusiig ashigladag*

2. **"Hearing and Obeying God"**
   - Бурханыг сонсож, дуулгавартай дагах
   - *Burkhanyg sonsoj, duulgavartai dagakh*

3. **"God's voice"**
   - Бурханы дуу хоолой
   - *Burkhany duu khooloi*

4. **"God's Story"**
   - Бурханы түүх
   - *Burkhany tuukh*

5. **"What do you learn about God?"**
   - Чи Бурханы талаар юу сурсан бэ?
   - *Chi Burkhany talaar yuu sursan be?*

6. **"God makes plans"**
   - Бурхан төлөвлөгөө гаргадаг
   - *Burkhan tuluuluuguu gargadag*

**Notes**:
- "Бурхан" is consistently used throughout the translation
- This term comes from Tibetan Buddhist tradition but has been adapted for Christian use
- Shows Russian influence in religious terminology
- The term maintains its Buddhist origins while being contextualized for Christian theology

   - *Yesusiin dagaldagch*

3. **"Jesus' commands"**
   - Есүсийн зарлигууд
   - *Yesusiin zarlig-uud*

4. **"obey Jesus"**
   - Есүсийг дуулгавартай дагах
   - *Yesusiig duulgavartai dagakh*

5. **"Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you"**
   - Есүсийг дуулгавартай дагаж, тантай хамт дагахыг бусдад зааж байна

**Notes**:
- Direct phonetic transliteration from Russian/Greek
- Consistently used throughout

### Holy Spirit
**Mongolian**: Ариун Сүнс (Ariun Suns)
**Transliteration**: Ariun Suns
**Literal Meaning**: Holy + Spirit/Soul

#### Examples in Context:
1. **"Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together"**
   - Бурханы Ариун Сүнснээс та бүхний цагийг хамтдаа удирдахыг хүс
   - *Burkhany Ariun Sunsneес ta bukhny tsagiig khamtdaa udirdakhyg khus*

2. **"God's Holy Spirit"**
   - Бурханы Ариун Сүнс
   - *Burkhany Ariun Suns*

**Component Analysis**:
- **Ариун** (Ariun) = Holy, sacred, pure
- **Сүнс** (Suns) = Spirit, soul (native Mongolian word)

**Notes**:
- Compound term combining loanword "Ariun" with native "Suns"
- Consistently used in Trinity formula

### Trinity Formula
**English**: "Father, Son, and Holy Spirit"
**Mongolian**: Эцэг, Хүү, Ариун Сүнс (Etseg, Khuu, Ariun Suns)

#### Full Context Example:
**"baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"**
- Эцэг, Хүү, Ариун Сүнсний нэрээр баптисм хүртэж
- *Etseg, Khuu, Ariun Sunsny nereer baptism khurtej*

**Component Analysis**:
- **Эцэг** (Etseg) = Father (native term)
- **Хүү** (Khuu) = Son (native term)
- **Ариун Сүнс** (Ariun Suns) = Holy Spirit (compound)

**Notes**:
- Uses native Mongolian kinship terms for Father and Son
- Maintains traditional Christian formula structure

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
**Mongolian**: Шавь (Shav')
**Transliteration**: Shav'
**Literal Meaning**: Apprentice, disciple (Buddhist term)
**Source**: Tibetan Buddhist tradition

#### Examples in Context:
1. **"Simple Definition of Disciple and Church"**
   - Шавь ба сүмийн энгийн тодорхойлолт
   - *Shav' ba sumiin engiin todorhoololt*

2. **"Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God"**
   - Шавь байна гэдэг нь бид Бурханаас сонсож, Бурханыг дуулгавартай дагадаг гэсэн үг
   - *Shav' baina gedeе ni bid Burkhan-aas sonsoj, Burkhanyg duulgavartai dagadag gesen ug*

3. **"Disciples and the Church"**
   - Шавь нар ба Сүм
   - *Shav' nar ba Sum*

4. **"What's a Disciple?"**
   - Шавь гэж юу вэ?
   - *Shav' gezh yuu ve?*

5. **"Being A Disciple"**
   - Шавь байх
   - *Shav' baikh*

6. **"Making Disciples"**
   - Шавь бэлтгэх
   - *Shav' beltgekh*

7. **"multiplying disciple"**
   - олшрох шавь
   - *olshrokh shav'*

8. **"Ask yourself, what does my disciple need"**
   - Миний шавь юу хэрэгтэй вэ гэж өөрөөсөө асуугаарай
   - *Minii shav' yuu kheregtei ve gezh uuruusuu asuugaarai*

9. **"disciple-making"**
   - шавь бэлтгэх
   - *shav' beltgekh*

**Notes**:
- Direct adoption from Buddhist terminology
- Used consistently for "disciple"
- Carries connotation of master-apprentice relationship
- The plural form adds "нар" (nar): "шавь нар"

### Obey / Obedience
**Mongolian**: Дуулгавартай дагах (Duulgavartai dagakh)
**Transliteration**: Duulgavartai dagakh
**Literal Meaning**: To follow with obedience/submission

#### Examples in Context:
1. **"Hearing and Obeying God"**
   - Бурханыг сонсож, дуулгавартай дагах
   - *Burkhanyg sonsoj, duulgavartai dagakh*

2. **"obey Jesus' commands"**
   - Есүсийн зарлигуудыг дуулгавартай дагах
   - *Yesusiin zarlig-uudiig duulgavartai dagakh*

3. **"Obeyed"** (as category)
   - Дагасан
   - *Dagasan*

4. **"Obey"** (imperative)
   - Дага
   - *Daga*

5. **"Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus"**
   - Есүсийг дуулгавартай дагаж, тантай хамт дагахыг бусдад зааж байна
   - *Yesusiig duulgavartai dagaj, tantai khamt dagakhyg busdad zaaj baina*

**Component Analysis**:
- **Дуулгавартай** (Duulgavartai) = with obedience, submissively
- **Дагах** (Dagakh) = to follow

**Simpler Form**:
- **Дагах** (Dagakh) alone can mean "obey" or "follow"

**Notes**:
- Compound phrase emphasizing submission and compliance
- Can be shortened to just "дагах" in simpler contexts
- Emphasizes both hearing and following

## Translation Patterns and Strategy

### Overall Translation Approach
The Mongolian Zume translation demonstrates a **balanced hybrid strategy** combining:

1. **Buddhist Terminology Adaptation** (40%)
   - Core terms borrowed from Tibetan Buddhist tradition
   - Examples: Бурхан (God), Шавь (disciple), Сүм (church), Судар (scripture)
   - Strategy: Contextualize existing religious vocabulary

2. **Russian Loanwords** (20%)
   - Introduced during Soviet era
   - Examples: Есүс (Jesus), Христ (Christ), Библи (Bible), Баптисм (baptism)
   - Strategy: Use internationally recognized transliterations

3. **Native Mongolian Terms** (30%)
   - Kinship terms: Эцэг (Father), Хүү (Son)
   - Action terms: Дагах (follow/obey), Залбирал (prayer), Хуваалцах (share)
   - Descriptive phrases: Сайн мэдээ (gospel = "good news")
   - Strategy: Use accessible, culturally resonant terms

4. **Modern Terminology** (10%)
   - Soviet-era educational terms: Сургалт (training)
   - Contemporary terms: Нийгэмлэг (community), Дасгалжуулагч (coach)
   - Strategy: Employ familiar modern vocabulary

### Key Translation Principles

#### 1. **Accessibility Over Theological Precision**
The translation prioritizes terms that everyday Mongolians can understand:
- "Сайн мэдээ" (good news) instead of more theological term for gospel
- "Дагалдагч" (follower) used interchangeably with "Шавь" (disciple)
- Descriptive phrases preferred over abstract concepts

#### 2. **Cultural Bridge Building**
Buddhist terminology serves as a bridge to Christian concepts:
- "Бурхан" originally meant "Buddha" but now adapted for "God"
- "Шавь" carries Buddhist master-apprentice relationship model
- "Сүм" connects to familiar religious building concept

#### 3. **Action-Oriented Language**
Emphasis on verbs and active participation:
- "Дуулгавартай дагах" (obediently follow) rather than abstract "obedience"
- "Залбирах" (to pray) as action
- "Хуваалцах" (to share) emphasized throughout

#### 4. **Minimal Theological Jargon**
Notable absence of complex theological terms:
- Limited use of salvation language
- No "atonement" or "redemption" terminology found
- Focus on discipleship and following rather than systematic theology

#### 5. **Russian Influence for Proper Nouns**
Names and specific religious terms follow Russian transliteration:
- Есүс (Jesus), Христ (Christ), Библи (Bible)
- Standard across post-Soviet Christian communities
- Ensures consistency with other Christian materials in Mongolia

### Compound Term Construction
Mongolian frequently uses compound constructions:
- **Possessive + Noun**: "Бурханы үг" (God's word)
- **Adjective + Noun**: "Сүнслэг амьсгал" (spiritual breathing)
- **Noun + Noun**: "Залбирлын мөчлөг" (prayer cycle)
- **Descriptive Phrases**: "Их Эзэний оройн зоог" (Lord's evening meal)

### Grammatical Patterns

#### Possessive Case (-ын/-ний)
Used extensively to show relationship:
- Бурханы (God's) - genitive of Бурхан
- Есүсийн (Jesus') - genitive of Есүс
- Сүмийн (church's) - genitive of Сүм

#### Action Nouns (-гч/-ч)
Create agent nouns from verbs:
- Дагалдагч (follower) from дагах (to follow)
- Удирдагч (leader) from удирдах (to lead)
- Итгэгч (believer) from итгэх (to believe)

#### Adjective Formation (-лэг)
Create adjectives from nouns:
- Сүнслэг (spiritual) from сүнс (spirit)

### Verb Forms
Multiple forms of key verbs appear:
- **Infinitive**: Залбирах (to pray)
- **Noun Form**: Залбирал (prayer)
- **Imperative**: Залбир (pray!)
- **Past Participle**: Залбирсан (prayed)

## Cultural and Religious Notes

### Historical Context

#### Buddhist Heritage
Mongolia has a 400+ year history of Tibetan Buddhism (since 16th century), which profoundly influences religious vocabulary:

1. **Borrowed Terms**:
   - **Бурхан** (Burkhan): Originally "Buddha," now adapted for Christian "God"
   - **Шавь** (Shav'): Buddhist disciple/apprentice relationship
   - **Сүм** (Sum): Buddhist temple/monastery
   - **Судар** (Sudar): Buddhist scripture

2. **Cultural Resonance**:
   - Master-disciple relationship (шавь) deeply familiar from Buddhist tradition
   - Temple-centered worship (сүм) provides context for "church"
   - Sacred texts (судар) concept already established
   - Prayer and meditation practices parallel

3. **Recontextualization Strategy**:
   - Terms retain familiar sound/feeling while receiving new Christian meaning
   - Reduces foreignness of Christian concepts
   - Provides cultural bridge for understanding
   - Risk: Buddhist connotations may create confusion

#### Soviet Era (1924-1990)
Seventy years of Soviet rule left lasting linguistic impact:

1. **Russian Loanwords**:
   - Proper names: Есүс (Jesus), Христ (Christ)
   - Religious terms: Библи (Bible), Баптисм (baptism)
   - Educational terminology: Сургалт (training)

2. **Secular Vocabulary**:
   - Modern terms: Нийгэмлэг (community), Бүлэг (group)
   - Administrative language: Удирдагч (leader), Төлөвлөгөө (plan)

3. **Practical Effect**:
   - Russian transliterations ensure consistency with other Christian materials
   - Younger Mongolians familiar with Russian-based religious terms
   - Soviet educational vocabulary adapted for training concepts

#### Traditional Mongolian Religion
Indigenous Tengerism (sky worship) also influences terminology:

1. **Тэнгэр** (Tenger):
   - Traditionally: "Sky/Heaven" and supreme deity
   - Now: Used for "heaven" in Christian context
   - Retains ancient spiritual resonance

2. **Native Spiritual Concepts**:
   - Сүнс (Suns): Spirit/soul (native Mongolian term)
   - Natural/accessible to all Mongolians
   - Free from foreign associations

### Contemporary Context (1990-Present)

#### Post-Soviet Religious Freedom
Since 1990, Christianity has grown in Mongolia:

1. **Translation Decisions**:
   - Balance between Buddhist terms and new Christian meanings
   - Russian loanwords for consistency
   - Native terms for accessibility

2. **Audience Considerations**:
   - Most Mongolians have Buddhist cultural background
   - Younger generation more exposed to Western concepts
   - Urban vs. rural linguistic differences
   - Need for terms that work across these contexts

### Terminology Challenges

#### 1. Buddhist Connotations
**Challenge**: Buddhist-origin terms carry specific theological baggage

**Example**: Бурхан (God)
- **Buddhist meaning**: Buddha, enlightened being, impersonal divine force
- **Christian meaning**: Personal, creator God, Trinitarian
- **Solution**: Consistent use in Christian contexts gradually reshapes meaning
- **Limitation**: May not convey personal relationship aspect clearly

**Example**: Шавь (Disciple)
- **Buddhist meaning**: Monk or student of Buddhist master, hierarchical
- **Christian meaning**: Follower of Jesus, mutual relationships
- **Advantage**: Familiar master-apprentice model
- **Limitation**: May overemphasize hierarchy

#### 2. Russian Loanwords
**Challenge**: Foreign sound may feel disconnected from Mongolian experience

**Example**: Есүс (Jesus)
- **Advantage**: Internationally consistent
- **Limitation**: Sounds foreign, no native meaning
- **Alternative considered**: None viable - proper name

**Example**: Библи (Bible)
- **Advantage**: Universally recognized
- **Limitation**: No connection to "sacred text" concept in Mongolian
- **Note**: "Судар" could theoretically be used but "Библи" is standard

#### 3. Concept Gaps
Some Christian concepts lack direct Mongolian equivalents:

**Example**: "Grace"
- Traditional Buddhism: Karma-based merit system
- Mongolian: No single word for "unmerited favor"
- Possible terms: Нигүүлсэл (mercy), Энэрэл (compassion)
- Solution in Zume: Term not emphasized; focus on relationships instead

**Example**: "Salvation"
- Traditional Buddhism: Liberation (from cycle of rebirth)
- Christian: Rescue from sin, relationship restoration
- Possible term: Аврал (rescue, deliverance)
- Note: Minimal in Zume materials - focus is on "following" rather than "being saved"

#### 4. Secular vs. Sacred
Some terms lack religious specificity:

**Example**: Удирдагч (Leader)
- Used in: Business, politics, education, church
- **Advantage**: Accessible, democratic feel
- **Limitation**: Doesn't convey spiritual authority
- **Alternative**: Could use more traditional terms for spiritual leaders
- **Zume choice**: Emphasizes servant leadership over hierarchical authority

### Translation Philosophy Insights

#### Principles Observed:

1. **Functional Equivalence Over Formal**
   - "Сайн мэдээ" (good news) rather than formal "gospel" term
   - Descriptive phrases preferred
   - Meaning prioritized over word-for-word translation

2. **Clarity Over Tradition**
   - Explains concepts in accessible language
   - Avoids obscure theological terminology
   - Uses everyday vocabulary

3. **Action Over Doctrine**
   - Emphasizes what disciples DO
   - Minimal abstract theological language
   - Practical, behavioral focus

4. **Relationship Over Institution**
   - "Дагалдагч" (follower) emphasizes personal relationship
   - "Энгийн сүм" (simple church) reduces institutional focus
   - Community and relationship language emphasized

#### Implications for Translators:

1. **Buddhist Terms**: Can be effective bridge if carefully contextualized
   - Provide clear Christian definition
   - Distinguish from Buddhist meanings
   - Use consistently in Christian contexts

2. **Native Terms**: Prioritize when available
   - More accessible to all Mongolians
   - Culturally resonant
   - Less foreign feel

3. **Simplification**: Appropriate for disciple-making contexts
   - New believers need accessible language
   - Complex theology can come later
   - Practical application emphasized

4. **Consistency**: Essential with Russian loanwords
   - Maintain compatibility with other Christian materials
   - Proper names should follow established transliterations
   - Technical terms (Bible, baptism) use standard forms

### Strengths of Mongolian Zume Translation

1. **Accessibility**: Everyday Mongolians can understand the language
2. **Cultural Bridge**: Buddhist terms provide familiar entry points
3. **Action Focus**: Emphasis on doing, not just knowing
4. **Simplicity**: Avoids theological jargon that would confuse new believers
5. **Consistency**: Follows established conventions for religious terms
6. **Native Feel**: Good use of Mongolian grammatical structures and native vocabulary

### Potential Concerns

1. **Buddhist Baggage**: Terms like Бурхан and Шавь carry Buddhist connotations
2. **Theological Depth**: Minimal systematic theology vocabulary
3. **Salvation Language**: Very limited explicit gospel/salvation terminology
