## Linguistic Patterns Expected in Azerbaijani

### Vowel Harmony
Azerbaijani follows vowel harmony rules typical of Turkic languages:
- Front vowels: e, ə, i, ö, ü
- Back vowels: a, ı, o, u
- Suffixes harmonize with root word vowels

### Agglutination
Azerbaijani is agglutinative - adding suffixes to roots:
- **İnanmaq** (to believe) → **İnanan** (believer) → **İnananlara** (to the believers)
- **Sevgi** (love) → **Sevgili** (beloved) → **Sevgilərə** (to the beloveds)

### Possessive Suffixes
- **-ım/-im/-um/-üm** (my): Tanrım (my God), İmanım (my faith)
- **-ın/-in/-un/-ün** (your): Atanız (your Father)
- **-ı/-i/-u/-ü** (his/her/its): İmanı (his/her faith)

### Plural Suffix
- **-lər/-lar**: Şagirdlər (disciples), Möminlər (believers)

### Case System
Azerbaijani has 6 grammatical cases:
- Nominative, Genitive, Dative, Accusative, Locative, Ablative

### Russian Loanwords

### Arabic/Persian Religious Terms

## Translation Philosophy Considerations

### Terminology Choices
Future translators will need to decide:

1. **Allah vs. Tanrı for "God"**
   - **Allah**: Continuity with Muslim audience, familiar term
   - **Tanrı**: Distinction from Islamic context, native Turkic word

2. **Arabic/Persian loanwords vs. Turkic equivalents**
   - Example: Məhəbbət (Arabic) vs. Sevgi (Turkic) for "love"

3. **Formal vs. colloquial register**
   - Religious texts typically use higher register
   - Discipleship materials may use more accessible language

4. **Muslim-background appropriate terminology**
   - Sensitive to majority Muslim audience
   - Using familiar Quranic terms (İsa, Məsih) vs. distinctly Christian terms

### Cultural Sensitivity
- Azerbaijan is secular but Muslim-majority
- Soviet legacy affects religious vocabulary
- Young generation more familiar with Western loanwords
- Regional variations (northern vs. southern dialects)
