# Spanish (Latin American) Translation Reference Guide (es)

**Source File:** `po_files/zume-es.po`
**Purpose:** Reference guide for maintaining consistent spiritual terminology in Spanish (Latin American) translations
**Date Analyzed:** 2026-01-16

---

## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Spanish translations as used in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy uses accessible, practical language tailored for Latin American Spanish speakers in a simple church/house church context. The approach emphasizes action-oriented discipleship over theological abstraction, using informal peer-level language to facilitate multiplication and networking.

---

## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus
- **Spanish:** "Jesús"
- **Notes:** Consistently translated with proper Spanish spelling. Uses "de Jesús" (of Jesus) for possessive forms rather than English apostrophe construction.
- **Examples:**
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands" → "Aprende a obedecer los mandamientos de Jesús"
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "Cómo seguir a Jesús"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "¿Qué es un seguidor de Jesús?"
  - "How do I obey Jesus and help others become followers with me?" → "¿Cómo obedezco a Jesús y ayudo a otros a ser seguidores junto conmigo?"
  - "Becoming more like Jesus... Together" → "Ser más como Jesús... juntos"

### Christ
- **Spanish:** "Cristo"
- **Notes:** Primarily appears in compound phrases rather than standalone
- **Pattern:** Most commonly seen in "cuerpo de Cristo" (Body of Christ)
- **Examples:**
  - "Pray and thank God for the Body of Christ" → "Ora y da gracias a Dios por el cuerpo de Cristo"

### Messiah
- **Spanish:** NOT FOUND
- **Notes:** This term does not appear in the translation, likely due to the practical/training nature of content rather than theological exposition

### God
- **Spanish:** "Dios"
- **Notes:** Used consistently throughout the translation
- **Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Dios se vale de personas comunes"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Escuchando y obedeciendo a Dios"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Ser discípulo significa escuchar a Dios y obedecerle"
  - "What do you learn about God?" → "¿Qué aprendes sobre Dios?"
  - "God makes plans, and He expects us to make plans, too" → "Dios tiene planes y espera que nosotros también los tengamos"
- **Variations:**
  - "God's Kingdom" → "el reino" (the kingdom - lowercase)
  - "God's voice" → "la voz de Dios"
  - "God's Story" → "la historia de Dios"

### Holy Spirit
- **Spanish:** "Espíritu Santo" or "Espíritu Santo de Dios"
- **Notes:** Always capitalized. Uses formal theological term. Sometimes includes "de Dios" (of God) for clarity.
- **Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "Pídele al Espíritu Santo que guíe este tiempo juntos"
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time, together" → "Pide al Espíritu Santo de Dios que guíe su tiempo juntos"

### Spirit / Spiritual
- **Spanish:**
  - "Espíritu" (spirit - noun)
  - "Espiritual" (spiritual - adjective)
  - "Espiritualidad" (spirituality)
- **Examples:**

### Lord

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
- **Spanish:** "discípulo" (singular), "discípulos" (plural)
- **Related terms:** "discípulos multiplicadores" (multiplying disciples)
- **Examples:**
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "Ser discípulo significa escuchar a Dios y obedecerle"
  - "What's a Disciple?" → "¿Qué es un discípulo?"
  - "multiplying disciples" → "discípulos multiplicadores"
  - "trained multiplying disciple" → "discípulo multiplicador formado"

  - "Discipleship Concepts" → "Conceptos de discipulado"

### Follower

## Key Translation Principles

### 1. Informal Peer-Level Language
The translation uses **informal Spanish ("tú" form)** consistently, appropriate for peer discipleship contexts:
- Uses informal "tú" (you) rather than formal "usted"
- Creates peer relationship rather than hierarchical teacher-student dynamic
- Occasional formal constructions appear but informal register dominates

### 2. Dynamic Equivalence over Literal Translation
Prioritizes natural Spanish expression over word-for-word translation:
- "God uses ordinary people" → "Dios se vale de personas comunes" (God makes use of ordinary people)
- "Prayer Cycle" → "La rueda de oración" (The wheel of prayer - more meaningful metaphor)
- Maintains meaning while adapting expression to Spanish idiom

### 3. Practical vs Liturgical Language
Strongly favors **accessible, action-oriented vocabulary**:
- "Simple church" → "iglesia sencilla" (uncomplicated) not "iglesia simple" (basic)
- "Lord's Supper" → "La cena del Señor" not "comunión" or "eucaristía"
- "Training" → "entrenamiento" (practical) not "capacitación" (formal)
- Emphasizes doable actions over theological abstractions

### 4. Latin American Vocabulary Choices
Uses vocabulary common in Latin America:
- "Entrenamiento" (training) - Latin American preference
- "Aprendiz" and "becario" (trainee) - Latin American terms
- "Entrenar" rather than exclusively "capacitar"
- "Computadora" patterns (not "ordenador" - European Spanish)

### 5. Metaphor Retention and Adaptation
Preserves English metaphors where they work in Spanish:
- "Duckling Discipleship" → "Discipulado del patito" (metaphor retained)
- "Spiritual Breathing" → "La respiración espiritual" (metaphor preserved)
- "Prayer Walking" → "Caminata de oración" (naturally adapted)

### 6. Movement-Focused Language
Supports multiplication and networking emphasis:
- "Multiplicadores" (multipliers) as key descriptor
