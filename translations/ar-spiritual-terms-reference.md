# Arabic Translation Reference Guide (ar)

**Source File:** `po_files/zume-ar.po`
**Purpose:** Reference guide for maintaining consistent spiritual terminology in Arabic translations
**Date Analyzed:** 2026-01-16

---

## Overview

This document catalogs key spiritual terms and their Arabic translations as used in the Zume training materials. The translation strategy uses **formal Christian Arabic terminology** designed for Arabic-speaking Christian audiences. This is NOT a Muslim-friendly or Islamic-contextualized translation - it employs standard Christian vocabulary including the Christian name for Jesus (يسوع - Yasu'), church terminology (كنيسة - kanisah), and explicit Christian sacramental language. The approach emphasizes practical discipleship and multiplication movements within a clearly Christian framework.

## Core Deity Terms

### Jesus
- **Arabic:** يسوع
- **Transliteration:** Yasu'
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **يسوع (Yasu')** - the Christian Arabic form derived from Greek Ἰησοῦς - NOT **عيسى (Isa)** which is the Quranic/Islamic form. This is THE major indicator that this is a Christian-specific translation for Christian audiences, not for Muslim-contextualized outreach.
- **Examples:**
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands" → "تعلموا كيفية طاعة أوامر يسوع"
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "ما هو تابع يسوع؟"
  - "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you" → "تعليم الآخرين أن يطيعوا ويتبعوا يسوع معكم"
  - "Becoming more like Jesus... Together" → "نصبح أكثر شبهًا بيسوع... سويًا"
  - "How do I obey Jesus and help others become followers with me?" → "كيف أطيع يسوع وأساعد الآخرين ليصبحوا أتباع معي؟"

### Christ / Messiah
- **Arabic:** المسيح
- **Transliteration:** Al-Masih (The Messiah/Christ)
- **Notes:** الم سيح (al-Masih) is shared between Christian and Islamic usage, but in this context clearly refers to Jesus Christ in the Christian theological sense, not just as a prophet
- **Examples:**
  - "Body of Christ" → "جسد المسيح" (jasad al-Masih)
  - "Mind of Christ" → "فكر المسيح" / "عقل المسيح"
  - "the gift of union with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ" → "هبة الاتحاد مع موت ودفن وقيامة المسيح"
  - "Christian lifestyle" → "حياة مسيحية" (hayah masihiyah)

### God
- **Arabic:** الله
- **Transliteration:** Allah
- **Notes:** الله (Allah) is the standard Arabic term for God used by both Christians and Muslims. This is normative for Arabic Christian texts and is not considered Islamic-specific. Arab Christians have used "Allah" for God since before the rise of Islam.
- **Examples:**
  - "God Uses Ordinary People" → "يستخدم الله أشخاصًا عاديين"
  - "Hearing and Obeying God" → "سماع وطاعة الله"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "أن نكون تلميذًا يعني أن نستمع من الله وأن نطيع الله"
  - "Sharing God's Story" → "مشاركة قصة الله"
  - "What do you learn about God?" → "ماذا تعلّمتم عن الله؟"
  - "God makes plans, and He expects us to make plans, too" → "يضع الله خططًا، ويتوقع منا أن نضع خططًا أيضًا"

### Holy Spirit
- **Arabic:** الروح القدس / روح الله القدوس
- **Transliteration:** Al-Ruh Al-Quds / Ruh Allah Al-Qudus
- **Notes:**
  - الروح القدس (the Holy Spirit) - standard form
  - روح الله القدوس (God's Holy Spirit) - more emphatic form
  - Always capitalized
  - Refers to Third Person of Trinity (Christian usage), distinct from Islamic understanding
- **Examples:**
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together" → "اطلبوا من الروح القدس أن يقود وقتكم معًا"
  - "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time, together" → "اسألوا روح الله القدوس أن يقود وقتكم معًا"
  - "The movement of the Holy Spirit for His people toward disciple-making" → "تحرك الروح القدس لشعبه نحو صناعة التلاميذ"
  - "Spiritual life and change are impossible without the Holy Spirit" → "الحياة الروحية والتغيير غير ممكنين بدون الروح القدس"
- **Trinitarian Formula:**
  - "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" → "اذْهَبُوا وَتَلْمِذُوا جَمِيعَ الأُمَمِ وَعَمِّدُوهُمْ بِاسْمِ الآب وَالابْنِ وَالرُّوحِ الْقُدُسِ"

### Spirit / Spiritual
- **Arabic:**
  - روح (spirit - noun)
  - روحي / روحية (spiritual - masculine/feminine adjective)
  - الروحي / الروحية (the spiritual)
  - روحيّين (spiritual - plural masculine)
- **Transliteration:** Ruh / Ruhi / Ruhiyah
- **Examples:**
  - "Spiritual Breathing" → "التنفس الروحي" (at-tanaffus ar-ruhi)
  - "Spiritual Economy" → "الاقتصاد الروحي"
  - "Spiritual Growth" → "النمو الروحي"
  - "Spiritual Practices" → "الممارسات الرّوحية"
  - "spiritual families" → "عائلات روحية" / "أسر روحية"
  - "Extended spiritual family" → "عائلة روحيّة ممتدّة"
  - "Spiritual producers, not just consumers" → "منتجين روحيّين وليس مستهلكين فقط"

### Lord
- **Arabic:** الرب / رب
- **Transliteration:** Ar-Rabb / Rabb
- **Notes:** Used both as title for God and in compound terms
- **Examples:**
  - "The Lord's Supper" → "عشاء الرب" / "العشاء الرباني"
  - "Celebrate the Lord's Supper" → "احتفلوا بعشاء الرب"
  - "Invite the Lord to guide you" → "دعوة الرب لإرشادكم"
  - "Is hearing and responding to the Lord really like breathing?" → "هل الاستماع والاستجابة للرب حقًا مثل التّنفس؟"
  - "Ask the Lord to guide you" → "اطلبوا من الرب"

### Father (God the Father)
- **Arabic:** الآب
- **Transliteration:** Al-Ab (The Father)
- **Notes:** Part of Trinitarian formula
- **Examples:**
  - "In the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit" → "بِاسْمِ الآب وَالابْنِ وَالرُّوحِ الْقُدُسِ"
  - "Always directed toward the work of His Father" → "دائمًا موجه نحو عمل أبيه"

## Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
- **Arabic:** تلميذ (singular), تلاميذ (plural)
- **Transliteration:** Tilmidh (singular), Talamidh (plural)
- **Notes:** Extremely frequent term (200+ contexts). Standard Arabic word for student/disciple.
- **Examples:**
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "تعريف بسيط للتلميذ والكنيسة"
  - "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God" → "أن نكون تلميذًا يعني أن نستمع من الله وأن نطيع الله"
  - "What's a Disciple?" → "ما هو التلميذ؟"
  - "Being A Disciple" → "أن تكونوا تلاميذًا"
  - "Disciples and the Church" → "التلاميذ والكنيسة"
- **Compound Terms:**
  - "Multiplying disciples" → "تلاميذ متضاعفين" / "التلاميذ المتضاعفين"
  - "Trained multiplying disciple" → "تلميذ مضاعَف مُدرَّب"
  - "What does my disciple need" → "ماذا يحتاج تلميذي"

### Disciple-making / Discipleship
- **Arabic:**
  - صنع التلاميذ (disciple-making - literal: making disciples)
  - التلمذة (discipleship - the state/practice of being a disciple)
  - مفاهيم التلمذة (discipleship concepts)
- **Transliteration:** Sun' at-talamidh / At-talmadha
- **Examples:**
  - "Making Disciples" → "صنع التلاميذ"
  - "Learn what ducklings have to do with disciple-making" → "تعلموا ما علاقة البطّ الصغير بصناعة التّلاميذ"
  - "Duckling Discipleship" → "تلمذة فرخ البطة" / "تلمذة فروخ البط"
  - "Discipleship Concepts" → "مفاهيم التلمذة"
  - "To saturate the world with multiplying disciples in our generation" → "لتغطية العالم بالتلاميذ المتضاعفين في جيلنا"
  - "Train disciples how to make disciples" → "دربوا التلاميذ على كيفية صنع التلاميذ"

### Follower
- **Arabic:** تابع (singular), أتباع / تابعين (plural)
- **Transliteration:** Tabi' (singular), Atba' (plural)
- **Examples:**
  - "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "ما هو تابع يسوع؟"
  - "What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?" → "ما هي أنشطة تابع يسوع؟"

### Follow / Following
- **Arabic:** اتباع (noun/gerund), يتبع (verb), اتبعوا (imperative)
- **Transliteration:** Ittiba' / Yattabi' / Ittabi'u
- **Examples:**
  - "How to Follow Jesus" → "كيفية اتباع يسوع"
  - "What if many friends, family and others start following Jesus with me?" → "ماذا لو بدأ العديد من الأصدقاء والعائلة وغيرهم في اتباع يسوع معي؟"

  - "Places where Jesus' followers are rare" → "الأماكن التي يكون فيها أتباع يسوع نادرين"

### Believer
- **Arabic:** مؤمن (singular), مؤمنون / مؤمنين (plural)
- **Transliteration:** Mu'min (singular), Mu'minun/Mu'minin (plural)
- **Related:** غير مؤمن (unbeliever)
- **Notes:** Same word used in Islamic contexts, but here clearly refers to Christian believers
- **Examples:**
  - "Share the 'Training Cycle' with a believer on your list of 100" → "شارك 'دورة التدريب' مع مؤمنٍ من قائمتكم المكونة من 100 شخص"
  - "Disciple, unbeliever, or unknown" → "تلميذ، غير مؤمن، أو غير معروف"
  - "Challenge five people you know who are believers" → "تحدي خمسة أشخاص على الأقلّ تعرفونهم من المؤمنين"
  - "Ordinary believers" → "المؤمنين العاديين"
  - "What would happen if new believers immediately began sharing and discipling others?" → "ماذا سيحدث إذا بدأ المؤمنون الجدد في مشاركة الآخرين وتلمذتهم على الفور؟"

### Obey / Obedience
- **Arabic:**
  - طاعة (obedience - noun)
  - أطيع (I obey), يطيع (he obeys), تطيع (you obey)
  - أطعت (obeyed - past), يطيعوا (they obey)

- **Transliteration:** Ta'ah (noun), Uti'/Yuti' (verb forms)
- **Examples:**
  - "Spiritual Breathing is Hearing and Obeying God" → "التنفس الروحي هو استماع وطاعة الله"
  - "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands" → "تعلموا كيفية طاعة أوامر يسوع"
  - "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus" → "تعليم الآخرين أن يطيعوا ويتبعوا يسوع"
  - "How do I obey Jesus?" → "كيف أطيع يسوع"
  - "Heard, Obeyed, Shared, Trained" → "سمعت، أطعت، شاركت، دربت"
  - "The Power of Obedient Faith" → "قوة الإيمان المطيع"

## Church Terms

### Church
- **Arabic:** كنيسة (singular), كنائس (plural)
- **Transliteration:** Kanisah (singular), Kana'is (plural)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Uses **كنيسة (kanisah)** - the traditional Christian Arabic term for church, NOT **جماعة (jama'ah - gathering/assembly)** which is sometimes preferred in Muslim-friendly contexts. This again indicates a Christian-specific, not Muslim-contextualized, translation.
- **Examples:**
  - "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "تعريف بسيط للتلميذ والكنيسة"
  - "What's a Church?" → "ما هي الكنيسة؟"
  - "Simple church" → "كنيسة بسيطة"
  - "My Churches" → "كنائسي"
  - "Church Name" → "اسم الكنيسة"
  - "Parent Church" → "الكنيسة الأم"
  - "Leading a Simple Church" → "قيادة كنيسة بسيطة"
  - "2 simple churches per 5,000 people" → "كنيستان بسيطتان لكل 5,000 شخص"
  - "When you think of a church, what comes to mind?" → "عندما تفكرون في الكنيسة، ما الذي يتبادر إلى ذهنك؟"
  - "A 3/3 Group is not just a small group but a Simple Church" → "مجموعة 3/3 ليست مجرد مجموعة صغيرة بل هي كنيسة بسيطة"

### Simple Church
- **Arabic:** كنيسة بسيطة
- **Transliteration:** Kanisah basitah
- **Notes:** Consistent terminology throughout for the core concept of simple/house churches
- **Examples:**
  - "Always report new simple churches" → "أبلِغ دائمًا عن الكنائس البسيطة الجديدة"

### Body of Christ
- **Arabic:** جسد المسيح
- **Transliteration:** Jasad al-Masih
- **Examples:**
  - "Pray and thank God for the body of Christ" → "صلوا واشكروا الله على جسد المسيح"

### Congregation / Assembly
- **Arabic:** NOT FOUND as separate terms
- **Notes:** The general term كنيسة (church) covers these concepts. Alternative جماعة (jama'ah) is NOT used.

## Gospel and Scripture Terms

### Gospel
- **Arabic:** الإنجيل / إنجيل
- **Transliteration:** Al-Injil / Injil
- **IMPORTANT NOTE:** الإنجيل (Injil) is the Arabic term for "Gospel" used by both Christians and Muslims. In Islamic usage, it refers to the revelation given to Jesus (Isa). In Christian usage, it refers to the Good News of Jesus Christ and the Gospel accounts. Context makes the Christian meaning clear.
- **Examples:**
  - "The Gospel" → "الإنجيل"
  - "Share the Gospel" → "مشاركة الإنجيل"
  - "The Gospel and How to Share It" → "الإنجيل وكيفية مشاركته"
  - "3-Circles Gospel Presentation" → "عرض الإنجيل بالدوائر الثلاث"
  - "Sharing the Gospel" → "مشاركة الإنجيل"
  - "Practice Sharing the Gospel" → "تمرنوا على مشاركة الإنجيل"
  - "I will share my story [testimony] and God's story [Gospel]" → "سأشارك قصتي [الشهادة] وقصة الله [الإنجيل]"
  - "There is no one 'best' way to tell God's story (also known as the Gospel)" → "ليس هناك طريقة واحدة 'الأفضل' لسرد قصة الله (المعروفة أيضًا باسم الإنجيل)"

### Bible

## Sacraments and Practices

### Baptism / Baptize
- **Arabic:**
  - المعمودية (baptism - noun)
  - معمودية (a baptism)
  - عمّد (baptized - verb, past tense)
  - يعمّد (baptizes - present)
  - تعميد (baptizing - gerund)
  - تعمّد (was baptized - reflexive)
- **Transliteration:** Al-Ma'mudiyah (noun), 'Ammad/Yu'ammid (verb)
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Baptism terminology is highly sensitive in Muslim contexts. The use of المعمودية (ma'mudiyah) is the standard Christian Arabic term derived from Syriac/Greek roots. This is explicit Christian vocabulary that clearly identifies Christian practice.
- **Examples:**
  - "Baptism and How To Do It" → "المعمودية وكيفية القيام به"
  - "Baptism" → "المعمودية"
  - "Have you ever baptized someone?" → "هل سبق لكم أن عمّدتم شخصًا ما؟"
  - "IMPORTANT REMINDER – Have you been baptized?" → "تذكير مهمّ - هل سبق وتعمَّدتم؟"
  - "Baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit" → "عَمِّدُوهُمْ بِاسْمِ الآب وَالابْنِ وَالرُّوحِ الْقُدُسِ"
  - "Are all disciples allowed to baptize others?" → "هل يُسمَح لجميع التّلاميذ بتعميد الآخرين؟"
  - "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them..." → "اذْهَبُوا وَتَلْمِذُوا جَمِيعَ الأُمَمِ وَعَمِّدُوهُمْ..."

### Prayer / Pray
- **Arabic:**
  - الصلاة (prayer - noun)
  - صلاة (a prayer)
  - صلوا / صلّوا (pray - imperative)
  - يصلي (he prays)
- **Transliteration:** As-Salah (noun), Sallu/Yusalli (verb)
- **IMPORTANT NOTE:** صلاة (salah) is the standard Arabic word for prayer used by both Christians and Muslims. In Islamic contexts, it specifically refers to the five ritual prayers. In Christian contexts, it means general prayer/supplication. Context distinguishes the meaning.
- **Examples:**
  - "How to Spend an Hour in Prayer" → "كيفية إمضاء ساعة في الصلاة"
  - "Prayer Walking" → "مسير الصلاة" / "صلاة المسير"
  - "The BLESS Prayer Pattern" → "نمط صلاة المباركة"
  - "The B.L.E.S.S. Prayer" → "صلاة البركة B.L.E.S.S"
  - "Prayer Cycle" → "دورة الصلاة"
  - "Strong Prayer Life" → "حياة صلاة قوية"
  - "Pray and thank God" → "صلوا واشكروا الله"
  - "Pray for someone in their presence" → "صلّوا من أجل شخص في حضوره"
  - "The Bible tells us that prayer is our opportunity to talk with and listen to God" → "يخبرنا الكتاب المقدس أن الصّلاة هي فرصتنا للتّحدّث مع والاستماع إلى الله"

### Communion / Lord's Supper
- **Arabic:** عشاء الرب / العشاء الرباني
- **Transliteration:** 'Asha' ar-Rabb / Al-'Asha' ar-Rabbani
- **Notes:**
  - عشاء الرب (Lord's Supper - literal)
  - العشاء الرباني (The Lord's/Lordly Supper)
  - Distinctly Christian terminology for Eucharist/Communion
- **Examples:**
  - "The Lord's Supper" → "عشاء الرب"
  - "The Lord's Supper and How To Lead It" → "العشاء الرباني وكيفية قيادته"
  - "Celebrate the Lord's Supper" → "احتفلوا بعشاء الرب"

### Worship
- **Arabic:** عبادة
- **Transliteration:** 'Ibadah
- **Notes:** While عبادة (worship) is used in both Christian and Muslim contexts, it does not appear frequently as a standalone term in this translation. The concept is present through actions like prayer, praise, thanksgiving, etc.

### Testimony
- **Arabic:** شهادة / شهادتكم
- **Transliteration:** Shahadah
- **IMPORTANT NOTE:** شهادة (shahadah) in Islamic contexts specifically refers to the Muslim declaration of faith ("There is no god but Allah and Muhammad is his messenger"). In Christian Arabic, it means testimony/witness. This is a shared term with very different meanings in each tradition. Context makes the Christian meaning clear.
- **Examples:**
  - "Prepare Your 3-Minute Testimony" → "أعدّ شهادة الثلاث دقائق"
  - "3-Minute Testimony" → "شهادة في ٣ دقائق"
  - "Three-Minute Testimony" → "شهادة في 3 دقائق"
  - "Practice sharing your testimony" → "مارسوا مشاركة شهادتكم"
  - "Share Your Testimony" → "شاركوا شهادتكم"
  - "Your story about your relationship with God is called your testimony" → "قصتكم عن علاقتكم مع الله تُسمى شهادتكم"

### Witness (verb and noun)
- **Arabic:** شهود (witnesses - plural), شاهد (witness - singular), نشهد (we witness/testify)
- **Transliteration:** Shuhud (plural), Shahid (singular)

## Key Translation Principles

### 1. Formal Christian Arabic Approach
The translation uses **standard, formal Christian Arabic terminology** throughout. This is NOT a Muslim-friendly or Islamic-contextualized translation.

**Key Indicators:**
- Uses يسوع (Yasu') NOT عيسى (Isa) for Jesus
- Uses كنيسة (kanisah) NOT جماعة (jama'ah) for church
- Uses المعمودية (ma'mudiyah) - explicit Christian baptism terminology
- Includes full Trinitarian formula with Father, Son, and Holy Spirit
- Uses المسيح (al-Masih) for Christ in Christian theological sense

### 2. Liturgical-Practical Balance
Strikes a balance between formal religious vocabulary and everyday language:
- **Liturgical/Theological:** معمودية (baptism), الإنجيل (gospel), الكنيسة (church)
- **Practical/Accessible:** تدريب (training), مشاركة (sharing), نمو (growth)
- More practical and action-oriented than highly liturgical
- Maintains theological precision

### 3. Evangelical/Non-Denominational Paradigm
**Clear Characteristics:**
- Emphasis on personal discipleship over institutional church
- Focus on multiplication and movements
- Simple church language rather than traditional ecclesiastical terminology
- Avoids traditional clergy titles (pastor, elder, apostle, prophet)
- Uses trainer/coach language instead
- Action-oriented (obey, train, multiply, go)
- Relational (follower, disciple, family)
- Movement-focused (generations, networks, multiplication)
- Non-hierarchical leadership terminology

### 4. Target Audience: Arabic-Speaking Christians
**PRIMARY AUDIENCE:** Arabic-speaking Christians (Middle Eastern, North African, or diaspora)

**Evidence:**
- No accommodation to Islamic sensibilities
- Uses Christian-specific terminology without explanation
- Assumes familiarity with Christian concepts
- Biblical quotations in standard Arabic Christian Bible format

**NOT Primarily For:**
- First-contact Muslim evangelism
- Muslim-background inquirers (too explicit)
- Interfaith dialogue contexts
- Highly Islamic cultural contexts requiring bridge vocabulary

### 5. Shared Vocabulary Strategy
Many terms are used by both Christians and Muslims in Arabic:

**SHARED TERMS (but Christian context makes meaning clear):**
- الله (Allah/God)
- صلاة (Salah/Prayer)
- إيمان (Iman/Faith)
- مؤمن (Mu'min/Believer)
- الإنجيل (Injil/Gospel) - different meanings
- بركة (Barakah/Blessing)
- سلام (Salam/Peace)

**DISTINCTLY CHRISTIAN:**
- يسوع (Yasu'/Jesus)
- المعمودية (Ma'mudiyah/Baptism)
- كنيسة (Kanisah/Church)
- الثالوث (Trinity)
- الابن (The Son)
- الروح القدس (Holy Spirit) - concept differs from Islam

### 6. Movement-Focused Vocabulary
Reflects CPM/DMM (Church Planting Movement/Disciple-Making Movement) values:
- **Multiplication** (مضاعفة/تضاعف) emphasized over addition
- **Training** (تدريب) as core activity
- **Obedience** (طاعة) emphasized
- **Generational thinking** (أجيال)
- **Network structures** (شبكات)
- Every-believer participation
- Simple church (كنيسة بسيطة) paradigm

### 7. Verb Forms and Grammatical Patterns
**IMPERATIVE MOOD:** Frequent use of direct commands
- "Pray and thank..." (صلوا واشكروا)
- "Ask God..." (اسألوا الله)
- "Train disciples..." (دربوا التلاميذ)

**PLURAL FORMS:** Predominantly addresses groups (you plural)
- Emphasizes community and group discipleship
- Reflects training-group context

**ACTIVE VOICE:** Prefers active over passive
- Action and agency emphasized
- Clear subjects performing actions

### 8. Honorifics Approach
**RELATIVELY EGALITARIAN:**
- Simple "Jesus" (يسوع) without elaborate titles
- "Lord" (الرب) used but not excessively
- "God" (الله) without constant honorific additions
- Focuses on relational terms (Father, followers) over royal/majestic language

**CONTRAST with Islamic Style:**
Islamic texts typically include "صلى الله عليه وسلم" (peace be upon him) after prophet names. This translation does NOT follow that pattern.

## Cultural and Religious Analysis

### Comparison to Quranic/Islamic Terminology

**JESUS:**
- **Christian (this text):** يسوع (Yasu') - from Greek Ἰησοῦς
- **Quranic:** عيسى (Isa) - Arabic form
- **SIGNIFICANCE:** This is THE major indicator that this is a Christian-specific translation for Christian audiences, not for Muslim outreach

**GOD:**
- **Both use:** الله (Allah)
- **NOTE:** Allah is simply the Arabic word for God, used by Arab Christians since before Islam

**GOSPEL:**
- **This text:** الإنجيل (Injil) - refers to New Testament gospel/good news of Jesus
- **Islamic usage:** الإنجيل refers to the revelation given to Prophet Isa, believed by Muslims to be corrupted

**HOLY SPIRIT:**
- **This text:** الروح القدس (Ruh al-Quds) - Third person of Trinity
- **Islamic usage:** روح القدس sometimes refers to angel Gabriel

**BAPTISM:**
- **This text:** المعمودية (Ma'mudiyah) - Christian sacrament
- **Islamic practice:** No equivalent; ritual washing is called وضوء (wudu)
- **SENSITIVITY:** Baptism is highly controversial in Muslim-to-Christian conversion contexts

**CHURCH:**

## Recommended Usage Guidelines

### DO:
- Use يسوع (Yasu') for Jesus consistently (NOT عيسى - Isa)
- Use كنيسة (kanisah) for church (NOT جماعة - jama'ah)
- Use المعمودية (ma'mudiyah) for baptism explicitly
- Maintain full Trinitarian language (Father, Son, Holy Spirit)
- Use الله (Allah) for God (standard for Arab Christians)
- Use تلميذ/تلاميذ (tilmidh/talamidh) for disciple/disciples
- Use مضاعفة/تضاعف (muda'afah/tada'uf) for multiplication
- Use تدريب (tadrib) for training
- Maintain practical, action-oriented language
- Use imperative mood for direct commands
- Address groups with plural forms
- Emphasize obedience (طاعة), multiplication, and training

### DON'T:
- Don't use Quranic name عيسى (Isa) for Jesus
- Don't soften Trinitarian theology or avoid "Son of God" language
- Don't substitute جماعة (jama'ah - gathering) for كنيسة (church)
- Don't avoid explicit Christian baptism terminology
- Don't add Islamic honorifics after Jesus' name (peace be upon him, etc.)
- Don't introduce traditional clergy titles (pastor, elder, apostle)
- Don't use highly liturgical or overly theological vocabulary
- Don't assume Muslim-friendly contextualization is needed
- Don't introduce systematic theological terms not in existing translation
- Don't use reproductive metaphors instead of multiplication (تضاعف)
