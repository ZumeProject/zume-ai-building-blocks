## Executive Summary

This reference guide documents the spiritual and ministry terminology that needs to be translated from English to Latvian for the Zume training materials. The source PO file (`zume-lv.po`) currently contains **1,347 untranslated entries** with a revision date of 2024-02-20.

**Key Context:**
- Latvian is a Baltic language with influences from German, Russian, and other neighboring languages
- Latvia has Lutheran, Catholic, and Orthodox Christian traditions
- Religious terminology may vary between traditional and contemporary usage
- Case system: Latvian has 7 grammatical cases (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, locative, vocative, instrumental)
- Latvian uses diacritics: ā, č, ē, ģ, ī, ķ, ļ, ņ, š, ū, ž

## Translation Strategy Recommendations

### 1. Core Principles
- **Consistency**: Use the same Latvian term throughout for each English concept
- **Accessibility**: Prefer widely understood terms over archaic religious language
- **Cultural Sensitivity**: Respect Latvia's diverse Christian heritage (Lutheran, Catholic, Orthodox)
- **Biblical Alignment**: Where possible, align with established Latvian Bible translations

### 2. Recommended Reference Materials
- **Latvian Bible Translation**: Latvijas Bībeles Biedrības (Latvian Bible Society) translation
- **Lutheran Tradition**: Church terminology from Latvian Evangelical Lutheran Church
- **Catholic Tradition**: Terms from Roman Catholic Church in Latvia
- **Contemporary Usage**: Modern Christian terminology used in Latvian evangelical churches

### 3. Linguistic Considerations
- **German Loanwords**: Historical influence from Baltic German may provide familiar religious terms
- **Russian Influence**: Soviet-era terminology may be recognized but consider contemporary alternatives
- **Native Baltic Terms**: Use indigenous Latvian words where they convey meaning clearly
- **Compound Words**: Latvian allows compound word formation for new concepts

## Translation Patterns and Insights

### 1. Word Formation Patterns

**Noun Formation:**
- **Agent Nouns (Doer):** Add suffix -tājs/-tāja (masc./fem.)
  - vadīt (lead) → vadītājs (leader)
  - sekot (follow) → sekotājs (follower)
  - kalpot (serve) → kalpotājs (servant)

- **Abstract Nouns:** Add suffix -ība, -šana
  - ticēt (believe) → ticība (faith)
  - paklausīt (obey) → paklausība (obedience)
  - vairot (multiply) → vairošana (multiplication)

**Adjective Formation:**
- Add suffix -īgs
  - ticība (faith) → ticīgs (faithful)
  - auglis (fruit) → auglīgs (fruitful)
  - grēks (sin) → grēcīgs (sinful)

### 2. Case Usage Patterns

**Genitive (Possessive):**
- Used extensively for "of" relationships
- "Dieva Valstība" (Kingdom of God)
- "Kunga vakariņas" (Lord's Supper)
- "mācekļu darīšana" (disciple-making)

**Dative (To/For):**
- Used with indirect objects, often with "to pray to," "to obey"
- "lūgt Dievam" (pray to God)
- "paklausīt Kungam" (obey the Lord)

**Locative (In/At):**
- Used for location, both physical and spiritual
- "ticēt Dievā" (believe in God)
- "lūgšanā" (in prayer)
- "Kristībā" (in baptism)

### 3. Prefix Patterns

**pa-:** Indicates thoroughness, completion
- klausīt (listen) → paklausīt (obey thoroughly)
- mainīt (change) → pamainīt (transform completely)

**iz-:** Indicates outward action, completion
- glābt (save) → izglābt (save/rescue completely)

**at-:** Indicates return, reversal
- griezties (turn) → atgriezties (turn back/repent)

### 4. German Loanword Influences

Historical Baltic German influence visible in:
- Religious terminology similar to German cognates
- "Kristus" (Christ) cf. German "Christus"
- "baznīca" (church) from Middle Low German "kerke"
- Compound word formation similar to German pattern

### 5. Translation Philosophy Recommendations

**Consistency over Variety:**
- Use same term throughout for each English concept
- Create glossary for translators
- Maintain consistency across all materials

**Accessibility:**
- Prefer contemporary terms where possible
- Explain technical theological terms
- Use concrete examples for abstract concepts

**Biblical Alignment:**
- Cross-reference Latvian Bible translations
- Use established biblical terminology
- Respect ecumenical usage when possible

**Cultural Sensitivity:**
- Recognize Latvia's mixed Christian heritage
- Avoid denominational bias where possible
- Use terms understood across traditions

## Practical Translation Tips

### 1. Gender Agreement
Latvian adjectives and participles must agree in gender, number, and case:
- "vienkāršs māceklis" (simple disciple - masculine)
- "vienkārša baznīca" (simple church - feminine)
- "vienkārši rīki" (simple tools - plural)

### 2. Compound Nouns
Create compounds in genitive + noun pattern:
- "Dieva Valstība" (God's Kingdom) - not "Diev Valstība"
- "mācekļu darīšana" (disciple-making) - genitive plural

### 3. Verb Aspects
Latvian has perfective and imperfective aspects:
- "darīt" (to do/make) - imperfective
- "izdarīt" (to complete doing) - perfective
- Choose based on completion vs. ongoing action

### 4. Formal vs. Informal
Consider audience formality level:
- Biblical terms more formal: "Kungs," "Svētais Gars"
- Contemporary terms more accessible: "Jēzus," "Dievs"
- Match tone of original English

### 5. Acronyms and Wordplay
English acronyms may not translate:
- **S.O.A.P.S.:** May need to create new Latvian acronym or use explanation
- **B.L.E.S.S.:** Consider "SVĒTĪ" or similar Latvian mnemonic
- Consult with native speakers for creativity

### 6. Cultural Contextualization
Adapt examples to Latvian culture while preserving meaning:
- References to American culture may need adjustment
- Measurement units (miles → kilometers)
- Currency references
- Cultural illustrations
