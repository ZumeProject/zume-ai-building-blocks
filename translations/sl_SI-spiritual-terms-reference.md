# Slovenian (sl_SI) Spiritual Terms Translation Reference Guide

## Overview

This reference guide documents how key spiritual and ministry terms are translated from English to Slovenian in the Zume training materials. It serves as a coaching resource for translators to maintain consistency and theological accuracy in future translation work.

**Language Pair**: English → Slovenian (sl_SI)
**Source**: `zume-sl_SI.po`
**Translation Context**: Christian discipleship training materials
**Date**: 2026-01-16

---

## Slovenian Language Characteristics

### Key Features Relevant to Translation:

1. **Case System**: Slovenian has 6 grammatical cases (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, locative, instrumental) that affect noun and adjective endings
2. **Dual Number**: Unique feature among Slavic languages - has singular, dual (2), and plural (3+) forms
3. **Gender**: Three grammatical genders (masculine, feminine, neuter)
4. **Catholic Heritage**: Strong influence from Catholic liturgical language on religious terminology
5. **Formality Levels**: Distinction between formal (vi) and informal (ti) address

These features create complexities in translation, particularly for spiritual terms that must work across different grammatical contexts.

## I. Core Deity Terms

### God
**Primary Translation**: **Bog** (masculine noun)

**Examples**:
- "God Uses Ordinary People" → "Bog deluje po navadnih ljudeh"
- "Hearing and Obeying God" → "Slišati Boga in mu biti poslušen"
- "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God." → "Biti učenec pomeni, da slišiš Boga in si mu poslušen."
- "What do you learn about God?" → "Kaj se naučimo o Bogu?"
- "God makes plans, and He expects us to make plans, too." → "Bog dela načrte in pričakuje, da jih bomo delali tudi mi."
- "God uses ordinary people doing simple things to make a big impact." → (translated but msgstr empty in some contexts)

**Case Declensions**:
- Nominative: Bog (God does...)
- Genitive: Boga (of God, God's)
- Dative: Bogu (to God, for God)
- Accusative: Boga (God as object - "slišiš Boga" = you hear God)
- Locative: o Bogu (about God)
- Instrumental: z Bogom (with God)

**Possessive Forms**:
- **Božji** (adjective) - God's, of God
  - "God's Story" → "Božja zgodba"
  - "God's Word" → "Božja beseda"
  - "God's command" → "Božja zapoved"
  - "God's Spiritual Economy" → "Božje duhovno gospodarstvo"
  - "God's voice" → "Božji glas"
  - "God's Kingdom" → "Božje kraljestvo"

**Notes**:
- The possessive adjective "Božji" agrees in gender, number, and case with the noun it modifies
- "Bog" is capitalized when referring to the Christian God
- Always masculine gender

- When emphasized as "God's Holy Spirit," uses full form "Božji Sveti Duh"

## II. Discipleship Terms

### Disciple
**Primary Translation**: **učenec** (masculine noun)

**Plural**: **učenci** (disciples)

**Examples**:
- "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God." → "Biti učenec pomeni, da slišiš Boga in si mu poslušen."
- "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church" → "Preprosta opredelitev pojmov učenec in cerkev"
- "Simple Definition of Disciple & Church" → "Preprosta opredelitev pojmov »učenec« in »cerkev«"
- "What's a Disciple?" → "Kaj je učenec?"
- "Being A Disciple" → "Kaj pomeni biti učenec"
- "Ask yourself, what does my disciple need" → "Vprašaj se: kaj potrebuje moj učenec?"
- "follower of Jesus" → "Jezusov učenec"
- "What is a follower of Jesus?" → "Kaj je Jezusov učenec?"
- "What are the activities of a follower of Jesus?" → "Kaj Jezusov učenec počne?"
- "Disciples and the Church" → "Učenci in cerkev"

**Contextual Variations**:
- "my disciple" → "moj učenec" (the person I'm discipling)
- "Jesus' disciple" → "Jezusov učenec" (follower of Jesus)
- "disciple of Jesus" → "učenec Jezusa" (alternative genitive construction)

**Case Declensions**:
- Nominative singular: učenec
- Genitive singular: učenca
- Dative singular: učencu
- Accusative singular: učenca
- Nominative plural: učenci
- Genitive plural: učencev

**Notes**:
- Literal meaning: "student" or "learner"
- Same root as "učiti" (to teach/learn)
- Masculine noun with standard declension pattern
- Preferred over "sledilec" (follower) in most contexts
- "Jezusov učenec" is the standard phrase for "follower of Jesus"

### Disciple-making / Making Disciples
**Primary Translation**: **delanje učencev** (making/doing disciples)

**Verb Form**: **delati učence** (to make disciples)

**Examples**:
- "Learn what ducklings have to do with disciple-making." → "Spoznaj, kaj imajo račke skupnega z delanjem učencev."
- "Learn what ducklings have to do with disciple-making" → "Spoznaj, kaj imajo račke skupnega z delanjem učencev"
- "I want to make disciples who multiply!" → "Želim si delati učence, ki se množijo!"
- "Duckling Discipleship – Leading Immediately" → (title for disciple-making concept)
- "entire spiritual families who multiply for generations to come" → (concept of making disciples who make disciples)

**Related Phrases**:
- "disciple who makes disciples" → "učenec, ki dela učence"
- "making disciples" → "delanje učencev"

**Notes**:
- Uses verb "delati" (to do/make) + accusative plural "učence"
- Gerund form: "delanje" (the making/doing of)
- Active, practical concept
- Not "vodenje" (leading) but "delanje" (making/doing)

### Multiplying Disciple
**Translation**: **učenec, ki dela učence** (disciple who makes disciples)

**Alternative**: **izurjen učenec, ki dela učence** (trained disciple who makes disciples)

**Examples**:
- "1 trained multiplying disciple per 5,000 in the United States" → "1 izurjen učenec, ki dela učence, na 5.000 ljudi v ZDA"
- "1 trained multiplying disciple per 50,000 globally" → "1 izurjen učenec, ki dela učence na 50.000 ljudi po svetu"
- "To saturate the world with multiplying disciples in our generation." → "Svet napolniti z učenci, ki delajo učence, v času naše generacije."
- "disciples who multiply" → "učenci, ki se množijo"

**Notes**:
- Descriptive phrase rather than single compound word
- "izurjen" = trained
- "ki dela učence" = who makes disciples (relative clause)
- Can also use "ki se množijo" (who multiply) for emphasis on reproduction

### Discipleship
**Primary Translation**: **učeništvo** (discipleship as abstract noun)

**Examples**:
- "What is one area of discipleship (reading/understanding the Bible, praying, sharing God's story, etc.) that you want to learn more about?" → "O katerem področju učeništva (branje/razumevanje Svetega pisma, molitev,..."
- "Discipleship Concepts" → (section title)
- "Duckling Discipleship" → (specific method name, kept as proper noun)
- "Vision for Discipleship" → (title)
- "Zume Discipleship Worldwide Progress Dashboard" → (title)

**Related Concepts**:
- "area of discipleship" → "področje učeništva"
- "developing essential discipleship skills" → (referenced)

**Notes**:
- Abstract noun formed from "učenec" (disciple)
- "-stvo" suffix denotes state/condition/system
- Similar to English "-ship" suffix

### Disciple-maker
**Translation**: Typically expressed as phrase rather than compound noun

**Concept**: "učenec, ki dela učence" (disciple who makes disciples)

**Notes**:
- Slovenian doesn't create compound noun like English "disciple-maker"
- Expresses concept through relative clause construction
- Focus on action (making disciples) rather than role title

- "Leading a Simple Church" → (title)
- "Practice Leading a Simple Church" → (activity)
- "Simple church vision map" → "Zemljevid vizije preprostih cerkva"

**Case Variations**:
- Nominative singular: preprosta cerkev
- Nominative dual: preprosti cerkvi (2 simple churches)
- Nominative plural: preproste cerkve (3+ simple churches)
- Genitive plural: preprostih cerkva (of simple churches)
- Accusative plural: preproste cerkve (direct object)

**Notes**:
- "preprosta" (simple/straightforward) agrees with feminine noun "cerkev"
- Dual form "preprosti cerkvi" used for "2 churches" - unique Slovenian feature
- Concept emphasizes simplicity and reproducibility

**Translation**: **moje cerkve** (possessive + plural)

**Example**:
- "My Churches" → "Moje cerkve"

## VI. Sacraments and Practices

### Baptism
**Noun**: **krst** (masculine noun)

**Verb**: **krstiti** (to baptize)

**Examples**:
- "Baptism and How To Do It" → "Krst in kako nekoga krstiti"
- "Baptism" → "Krst"
- "Have you ever baptized someone?" → "Si že kdaj koga krstil?"
- "If the Great Commission is for every follower of Jesus, does that mean every follower is allowed to baptize others?" → "Če veliko poslanstvo velja za vsakega Jezusovega učenca, ali to pomeni, da sme vsak učenec krstiti druge?"
- "IMPORTANT REMINDER – Have you been baptized?" → "POMEMBEN OPOMNIK – Si že krščen?"
- "How to Baptize" → (title)
- "baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" → (Trinitarian formula)
- "If you know anyone who follows Jesus but has not been baptized, challenge them to do so and offer to baptize them." → "Če poznaš koga, ki sledi Jezusu, a še ni bil krščen, ga pozovi, naj to stori. Ponudi, da ga krstiš ti."

**Verb Forms**:
- Infinitive: krstiti (to baptize)
- Past participle masculine: krstil (he baptized)
- Past participle passive: krščen (baptized - as adjective)
- Infinitive phrase: "kako nekoga krstiti" = how to baptize someone

**Case Declensions (noun)**:
- Nominative: krst
- Genitive: krsta
- Accusative: krst

**Related Phrases**:
- "to baptize someone" → "krstiti koga" / "nekoga krstiti"
- "baptized" (passive/state) → "krščen"
- "have been baptized" → "si krščen" (past state)
- "has not been baptized" → "ni bil krščen"

**Notes**:
- Verb "krstiti" is imperfective
- Passive participle "krščen" used for baptismal state
- "krst" also means "cross" in some contexts - context determines meaning
- Traditional Catholic sacramental terminology

## XIV. Translation Patterns and Strategies

### 1. **Descriptive Phrases vs. Compound Words**

Slovenian often uses descriptive phrases where English uses compound nouns:

- "disciple-making" → "delanje učencev" (making of disciples)
- "disciple-maker" → "učenec, ki dela učence" (disciple who makes disciples)
- "multiplying disciple" → "učenec, ki dela učence" (disciple who makes disciples)
- "vision casting" → "deljenje vizije" (sharing of vision)
- "prayer walking" → "molitveni sprehod" (prayer walk) OR "molitev med sprehajanjem" (prayer during walking)

**Pattern**: Noun + genitive construction OR relative clause construction

### 2. **Verbal Nouns (Gerunds)**

Slovenian frequently uses verbal nouns ending in -anje/-enje:

- "sharing" → "deljenje" (from "deliti" = to share)
- "training" → "urjenje" (from "uriti" = to train)
- "coaching" → "mentoriranje" (from "mentorirati" = to coach)
- "reading" → "branje" (from "brati" = to read)
- "studying" → "preučevanje" (from "preučevati" = to study)
- "equipping" → "opremljanje" (from "opremiti" = to equip)
- "multiplication" → "množenje" (from "množiti" = to multiply)

**Pattern**: Verb stem + -anje/-enje suffix

### 3. **Possessive Adjectives**

Slovenian uses possessive adjectives that agree in gender, number, and case:

- "God's" → "Božji" (masculine), "Božja" (feminine), "Božje" (neuter)
- "Jesus'" → "Jezusov" (masculine), "Jezusova" (feminine), "Jezusovo" (neuter)
- "Lord's" → "Gospodov" (masculine), "Gospodova" (feminine), "Gospodovo" (neuter)

**Pattern**: Possessive adjective must agree with the noun it modifies

### 4. **Case System Usage**

The six-case system creates variations:

**Genitive (of, from)**:
- "učenec" → "učenca" (of a disciple)
- "delanje učencev" (making of disciples)
- "deljenje evangelija" (sharing of gospel)

**Dative (to, for)**:
- "poslušen Bogu" (obedient to God)
- "poslušni Jezusu" (obedient to Jesus)

**Locative (in, at, about)**:
- "v molitvi" (in prayer)
- "o Bogu" (about God)

**Instrumental (with, by)**:
- "z učenci" (with disciples)
- "s prijateljem" (with a friend)

### 5. **Borrowed Terms vs. Native Terms**

The translation uses both borrowed international terms and native Slovenian words:

**Borrowed Terms**:
- "evangelij" (gospel) - from Greek
- "Biblija" (Bible) - from Greek
- "multiplikacija" (multiplication) - from Latin
- "mentor" (mentor/coach) - from French/Latin
- "generacija" (generation) - from Latin
- "vizija" (vision) - from Latin

**Native Alternatives Sometimes Used**:
- "Sveto pismo" (Holy Scripture) preferred over "Biblija"
- "množenje" (native) alongside "multiplikacija" (borrowed)
- "Božja zgodba" (God's story) alongside "evangelij" (gospel)

**Pattern**: Formal/liturgical contexts prefer traditional terms; conversational contexts may use native alternatives

### 6. **Dual Number Forms**

Slovenian's unique dual number appears in the text:

- "2 simple churches" → "2 preprosti cerkvi" (dual form)
- Compare with plural: "3+ simple churches" → "preproste cerkve"

**Pattern**: Number 2 triggers dual forms distinct from singular and plural

### 7. **Reflexive Verbs**

Many spiritual action verbs are reflexive:

- "to multiply" → "množiti se" (to multiply oneself)
- "to reproduce" → "razmnoževati se"
- "is training" → "se uri" (is training himself/herself)

**Pattern**: Reflexive particle "se" used for self-directed action

### 8. **Action-Oriented Language**

The translation emphasizes active participation over passive reception:

- "making disciples" → "delanje učencev" (doing/making disciples)
- "practice obeying" → "vaditi poslušnost" (practice obedience)
- "to be obedient" → "biti poslušen" (state of obedience)

**Pattern**: Emphasis on concrete actions and practices

### 9. **Catholic Liturgical Influence**

Traditional Catholic terminology is standard:

- "Sveti Duh" (Holy Spirit) - matches liturgy
- "Gospodova večerja" (Lord's Supper) - traditional term
- "Sveto pismo" (Holy Scripture) - formal religious term
- "krst" (baptism) - sacramental terminology

**Pattern**: Religious vocabulary follows Catholic tradition even in Protestant discipleship context

### 10. **Contextual Adaptation**

Some terms are culturally adapted:

- "Generational mapping" → "Generacijsko drevo" (generational tree) - uses tree metaphor
- "Accountability Groups" → "Skupine vzajemnega mentorstva" (peer mentoring groups) - reframes concept
- "List of 100" → (concept referenced but not formally translated)

**Pattern**: Cultural adaptation when direct translation wouldn't communicate clearly

## XVI. Usage Notes for Translators

### 1. **Consistency in Deity References**

**Always capitalize and use standard forms**:
- Bog (God) - not bog
- Jezus (Jesus) - not jezus
- Sveti Duh (Holy Spirit) - both words capitalized
- Gospod (Lord) - capitalized

### 2. **Case Agreement is Critical**

Ensure possessive adjectives agree with nouns:
- "Božji glas" (God's voice - masculine)
- "Božja beseda" (God's word - feminine)
- "Božje kraljestvo" (God's kingdom - neuter)

### 3. **Choose Appropriate Register**

- Formal/liturgical: "Sveto pismo" (Holy Scripture)
- Conversational: "Božja zgodba" (God's story)

Match register to context and audience.

### 4. **Maintain Action Orientation**

Prefer active, practical language:
- "delanje učencev" (making disciples) over abstract concepts
- "vaditi poslušnost" (practice obedience) over theory
- Verbal nouns (-anje/-enje) for ongoing actions

### 5. **Use Dual Forms Correctly**

Remember Slovenian's unique dual number:
- 1 church: ena cerkev
- 2 churches: dve cerkvi (dual)
- 3+ churches: tri cerkve (plural)

### 6. **Balance Borrowed and Native Terms**

Use borrowed international terms when:
- They're widely understood (evangelij, mentor, vizija)
- Technical/theological precision needed

Use native terms when:
- More accessible (Božja zgodba for evangelij)
- More concrete (deljenje for "casting")

### 7. **Relative Clauses for Compound Concepts**

For English compound nouns, use Slovenian relative clauses:
- "disciple who makes disciples" → "učenec, ki dela učence"
- "churches that multiply" → "cerkve, ki se množijo"

### 8. **Reflexive Verbs for Multiplication**

Use reflexive forms for self-propagating concepts:
- "množiti se" (to multiply - reflexive)
- "razmnoževati se" (to reproduce - reflexive)

### 9. **Gender Consistency**

Track gender through declensions:
- cerkev (church) - feminine throughout
- učenec (disciple) - masculine throughout
- kraljestvo (kingdom) - neuter throughout

### 10. **Cultural Sensitivity**

Remember Slovenian Catholic heritage:
- Use traditional liturgical terms appropriately
- Don't force Protestant English patterns
- Adapt concepts (like "accountability" → "vzajemno mentorstvo")
