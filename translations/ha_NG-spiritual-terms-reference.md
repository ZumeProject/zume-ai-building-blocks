# Hausa (Nigeria) Spiritual Terms Reference
## Comprehensive Translation Analysis from Zúme Training Materials

**Language:** Hausa (ha_NG)
**Region:** Northern Nigeria
**Script:** Latin script with extended characters (ɓ, ɗ, ƙ, etc.)
**Language Family:** Afroasiatic (Chadic branch)
**Religious Context:** Predominantly Muslim population with Christian minority
**Date Compiled:** 2026-01-16

---

## INTRODUCTION: LINGUISTIC AND CULTURAL CONTEXT

### Key Characteristics of Hausa Christian Terminology

1. **Arabic Loanwords**: Hausa has extensive Arabic vocabulary due to centuries of Islamic influence
2. **Muslim-Majority Context**: Christian terminology must navigate a predominantly Islamic linguistic landscape
3. **Shared Vocabulary**: Many religious terms are borrowed from Arabic and used by both Muslims and Christians
4. **Chadic Features**: Native Hausa grammatical structures with tonal distinctions
5. **Latin Script**: Modern Hausa uses Latin alphabet with special characters (ɓ, ɗ, ƙ, etc.)

## CORE DEITY TERMS

### 1. GOD / ALLAH
**Hausa:** Allah
**Script:** Allah
**Pronunciation:** ah-LAH
**Etymology:** Arabic loanword, shared by Muslims and Christians
**Grammatical Notes:** Invariable noun, no plural

#### Contextual Examples:
1. **English:** "God Uses Ordinary People"
   **Hausa:** "Allah Yana Amfani da Al'ummah"
   *Literal: Allah uses ordinary people*

2. **English:** "Spiritual Breathing is Hearing and Obeying God"
   **Hausa:** "Buguwa ta Ruhaniya ji ne da Biyayya ga Allah"
   *Literal: Spiritual breathing is hearing and obedience to Allah*

3. **English:** "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God"
   **Hausa:** "Kasancewa almajiri yana nufin munji daga Allah kuma munyi biyayya ga Allah"
   *Literal: Being a disciple means we hear from Allah and we obey Allah*

4. **English:** "Sharing God's Story"
   **Hausa:** "Raba Labarin Allah"
   *Literal: Sharing the story of Allah*

5. **English:** "What do you learn about God?"
   **Hausa:** "Me kuke koya game da Allah?"
   *Literal: What are you learning about Allah?*

**Cultural Notes:**
- The use of "Allah" is standard in Hausa Christian literature
- No alternative Christian-specific term for God is used
- This shared terminology facilitates communication across religious boundaries
- Reflects the linguistic reality that Arabic loanwords dominate religious vocabulary

### 2. JESUS / YESU
**Hausa:** Yesu
**Script:** Yesu
**Pronunciation:** YEH-soo
**Etymology:** Transliteration from English/Latin tradition
**Grammatical Notes:** Proper noun, invariable

#### Contextual Examples:
1. **English:** "Jesus is our measurement"
   **Hausa:** "Yesu ne gwargwadon mu"
   *Literal: Jesus is our standard/measurement*

2. **English:** "What is a follower of Jesus?"
   **Hausa:** "Menene mai bin Yesu?"
   *Literal: What is a follower of Jesus?*

3. **English:** "How to Follow Jesus"
   **Hausa:** "Yadda za'a Bi Yesu"
   *Literal: How to follow Jesus*

4. **English:** "Learn how to obey Jesus' commands"
   **Hausa:** "Koyi yadda zaka yi biyayya da dokokin Yesu"
   *Literal: Learn how you will obey the laws/commands of Jesus*

5. **English:** "Teaching others to obey and follow Jesus with you"
   **Hausa:** "Koyar da wasu suyi biyayya da bin Yesu tare da kai"
   *Literal: Teaching others they obey and follow Jesus together with you*

**Phrase Patterns:**
- "mai bin Yesu" = follower of Jesus (lit: one who follows Jesus)
- "mabiyan Yesu" = followers of Jesus (plural)
- "bin Yesu" = following Jesus
- "dokokin Yesu" = Jesus' commands/laws

**Cultural Notes:**
- "Yesu" is the standard Christian transliteration
- Muslims may use "Isa" (from Arabic) for Jesus as a prophet
- The use of "Yesu" marks Christian context

### 3. HOLY SPIRIT / RUHU MAI TSARKI
**Hausa:** Ruhu Mai Tsarki
**Script:** Ruhu Mai Tsarki
**Pronunciation:** ROO-hoo mai TSAR-kee
**Etymology:** Ruhu (Arabic: spirit/soul) + Mai (Hausa possessive) + Tsarki (Hausa: holy/sacred)
**Grammatical Notes:** Compound noun phrase; Mai = possessive marker meaning "one who has"

#### Contextual Examples:
1. **English:** "Ask God's Holy Spirit to lead your time together"
   **Hausa:** "Tambayi Ruhu Mai Tsarki na Allah ya jagoranci lokacinku tare"
   *Literal: Ask the Holy Spirit of Allah to lead your time together*

2. **English:** "Jesus said, 'You will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you'"
   **Hausa:** "Yesu ya ce, 'Za ku karɓi iko lokacin da Ruhu Mai Tsarki ya sauko muku'"
   *Literal: Jesus said, 'You will receive power when the Holy Spirit descends upon you'*

3. **English:** "Jesus said, 'Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit'"
   **Hausa:** "Yesu ya ce, 'Ku je ku almajirtar da dukkan al'ummai, KARANTA su da sunan Faw kuma da Da da Ruhu Mai Tsarki'"
   *Literal: Jesus said, 'Go disciple all nations, baptizing them in the name of Father and also of Son and Holy Spirit'*

4. **English:** "The Last 100 Hours Map shows the Holy Spirit moving His people"
   **Hausa:** "Taswirar Sa'o'i 100 na Ƙarshe yana nuna Ruhu Mai Tsarki yana motsa mutanensa"
   *Literal: The map of last 100 hours shows the Holy Spirit moving His people*

**Component Analysis:**
- **Ruhu** = spirit, soul (Arabic origin: ruh)
- **Mai** = possessive particle (one who has/possesses)
- **Tsarki** = holiness, sacredness (native Hausa)

**Cultural Notes:**
- Compound phrase construction typical of Hausa
- Combines Arabic loanword with native Hausa term
- "Mai Tsarki" literally means "the one possessing holiness"

### 4. LORD / UBANGIJI
**Hausa:** Ubangiji
**Script:** Ubangiji
**Pronunciation:** oo-bahn-GEE-jee
**Etymology:** Native Hausa compound: Uba (father) + n (of) + gida (house/household)
**Literal Meaning:** "Father of the house" or "Master of the household"
**Grammatical Notes:** Noun, can be used as title or reference

#### Contextual Examples:
1. **English:** "The Lord's Supper and How To Lead It"
   **Hausa:** "Jibin Maraice na Ubangiji da Yadda za'a bishi"
   *Literal: The evening meal of the Lord and how to lead it*

2. **English:** "The Lord's Supper"
   **Hausa:** "Jibin Maraice na Ubangiji"
   *Literal: The evening meal of the Lord*

3. **English:** "Celebrate the Lord's Supper"
   **Hausa:** "Yi bikin Jibin Ubangiji"
   *Literal: Celebrate the meal of the Lord*

**Etymology Details:**
- **Uba** = father (native Hausa)
- **n** = possessive connector
- **gida** = house, household (implicit in compound)
- Full meaning: patriarch, master, lord of the household

**Cultural Notes:**
- Native Hausa term, not Arabic loanword
- Carries connotations of authority, patriarchy, and protection
- Used in both Islamic and Christian contexts
- Reflects traditional Hausa social structure with male household heads

### 5. FATHER (GOD THE FATHER) / UBA
**Hausa:** Uba
**Script:** Uba
**Pronunciation:** OO-bah
**Etymology:** Native Hausa term for father
**Grammatical Notes:** Noun; can be possessed (Ubana = our Father)

#### Contextual Examples:
1. **English:** "Baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"
   **Hausa:** "KARANTA su da sunan Faw kuma da Da da Ruhu Mai Tsarki"
   *Note: Uses "Faw" (Father) borrowed from English pronunciation*

2. **English:** "In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"
   **Hausa:** "cikin sunan Uba, da Ɗa, da na Ruhu Mai Tsarki"
   *Literal: in the name of Father, and Son, and of the Holy Spirit*

**Variations:**
- **Uba** = Father (general)
- **Faw** = Father (borrowed from English, used in liturgical contexts)
- **Ubangiji** = Lord (lit: Father of the house)

**Cultural Notes:**
- "Uba" is the native Hausa term
- "Faw" appears as English loanword in Trinitarian formula
- Both forms coexist in Christian usage

## DISCIPLESHIP TERMS

### 1. DISCIPLE / ALMAJIRI
**Hausa:** Almajiri (singular), Almajirai (plural)
**Script:** Almajiri, Almajirai
**Pronunciation:** ahl-mah-JEE-ree, ahl-mah-jee-RAI
**Etymology:** Arabic loanword from "al-muhajirun" (those who migrate for learning)
**Traditional Context:** Originally referred to Islamic students/learners

#### Contextual Examples:
1. **English:** "Simple Definition of Disciple and Church"
   **Hausa:** "Ma'anar Ka'idar Ka'ida da Coci" / "Sauƙaƙan Ma'anar Almajiri & Coci"
   *Literal: Simple meaning of disciple & church*

2. **English:** "Being a disciple means we hear from God and we obey God"
   **Hausa:** "Kasancewa almajiri yana nufin munji daga Allah kuma munyi biyayya ga Allah"
   *Literal: Being a disciple means we hear from Allah and we obey Allah*

3. **English:** "Ask yourself, what does my disciple need"
   **Hausa:** "Ka tambayi kanka, menene almajirina yake bukata"
   *Literal: Ask yourself, what does my disciple need*

4. **English:** "1 trained multiplying disciple per 5,000 in the United States"
   **Hausa:** "1 ya horar da almajirai masu yawa a cikin 5,000 a Amurka"
   *Literal: 1 trained disciples multiplying within 5,000 in America*

5. **English:** "To saturate the world with multiplying disciples in our generation"
   **Hausa:** "Domin cika duniya da almajirai masu yawa a cikin tsararrakinmu"
   *Literal: To fill the world with multiplying disciples in our generation*

**Forms and Variations:**
- **almajiri** = disciple (singular)
- **almajirai** = disciples (plural)
- **almajirina** = my disciple (possessive)
- **almajirantarwa** = discipleship, disciple-making (verbal noun)

**Verb Forms:**
- **yi almajirai** = make disciples
- **almajirtar** = to disciple, to make disciples

**Cultural Notes:**
- Originally an Islamic term for religious students
- Appropriated by Christians for "disciple"
- Carries connotations of learner, student, follower
- In traditional Hausa context, almajirai were itinerant Quranic students
- Christian usage recontextualizes the term for followers of Jesus

6. **English:** "2 simple churches per 5,000 people in the United States"

**Cultural Notes:**

- Christians distinguish their "coci" from Islamic "masallaci" (mosque)

## GOSPEL AND SCRIPTURE TERMS

### 1. GOSPEL / BISHARA, INJILA
**Hausa:** Bishara (primary), Injila / Linjila (alternate)
**Script:** Bishara, Injila, Linjila
**Pronunciation:** bee-SHAH-rah, een-JEE-lah

1. **English:** "The Gospel and How to Share It"
   **Hausa:** "Bishara da Yadda ake Rarraba shi"
   *Literal: Gospel and how to share it*

2. **English:** "3-Circles Gospel Presentation"
   **Hausa:** "3- Gabatarwar Injila" / "Gabatarwar Linjila 3 Circles"
   *Literal: 3 circles gospel presentation*

3. **English:** "The Gospel"
   **Hausa:** "Bishara"

4. **English:** "Share the Gospel"
   **Hausa:** "Raba Bishara"
   *Literal: Share the gospel*

5. **English:** "Take turns telling the Gospel to each other"
   **Hausa:** "Ku bi da bi suna gaya wa juna bishara"
   *Literal: Take turns telling each other the gospel*

6. **English:** "Sharing the Gospel"
   **Hausa:** "Rabawa da Bishara"
   *Literal: Sharing with gospel*

## GRAMMATICAL PATTERNS AND LINGUISTIC NOTES

### Possessive Constructions
1. **Mai + Noun** = "one who has [noun]"
   - Mai Tsarki = Holy One (one who has holiness)
   - Mai bin Yesu = follower of Jesus (one who follows Jesus)
   - Mai aminci = faithful one (one who has faithfulness)

2. **Na/Ta + Noun** = "of [noun]" (genitive)
   - Ruhu Mai Tsarki na Allah = Holy Spirit of Allah
   - Jibin na Ubangiji = Supper of the Lord
   - Labarin Allah = Story of Allah

3. **Noun + -n/-r** = possessive suffix
   - Amincin = faithfulness (the faithfulness)
   - Kocin = coaching (the coaching)
   - Shaidar = testimony of

### Verb Constructions
**Yi + Noun** = "to do [noun]" (common verb pattern)
- Yi biyayya = to obey (do obedience)
- Yi addu'a = to pray (do prayer)
- Yi baftisma = to baptize (do baptism)
- Yi imani = to believe (do faith)
- Yi hidima = to serve (do service)

### Agentive Nouns
**Mai/Masu** = "one who/those who"
- Mai bin = follower (one who follows)
- Mabiyan = followers (plural)
- Mai horarwa = trainer (one who trains)
- Masu yawa = many, multiplying (those who are many)

### Verbal Nouns (Gerunds)
Many Hausa verbs form gerunds with suffixes:
- **-wa/-rwa**: Horarwa (training), Koyarwa (teaching), Jagoranci (leading)
- **-n/-r**: Bin (following), Biyayya (obedience)

### Pluralization
Regular plurals often formed with:
- **-ai/-oi**: Almajirai (disciples), Ikklisiyoyi (churches)
- **-u**: Majami'u (congregations), Nassosi (scriptures)
- **-anni**: Shugabanni (leaders)

### Special Characters
Hausa uses Latin script with implosive and ejective consonants:
- **ɓ** = implosive b
- **ɗ** = implosive d
- **ƙ** = ejective k (like Arabic qaf)

## CULTURAL AND MISSIOLOGICAL NOTES

### 1. Islamic-Christian Vocabulary Overlap
The extensive use of Arabic loanwords creates both opportunities and challenges:

**Shared Terms:**
- Allah (God)
- Addu'a (prayer)
- Ruhu (spirit)
- Imani (faith)
- Mumini (believer)
- Albarka (blessing)
- Al'umma (community)

**Implications:**
- Facilitates dialogue with Muslim-background seekers
- Requires careful contextualization to distinguish Christian meanings
- Some terms (like Almajiri) carry specific Islamic educational connotations

### 2. Christian-Specific Adaptations
- **Yesu** (Jesus) vs. **Isa** (Islamic name for Jesus)
- **Baftisma** (baptism) - uniquely Christian practice
- **Coci/Ikklisiya** (church) vs. **Masallaci** (mosque)
- **Kristi** (Christ) - theological title

### 3. Linguistic Strategies
The translation employs several contextualization strategies:

**Borrowed Terms:**
- English loanwords: Coci (church), Koci (coach), Bishara/Injila (gospel)
- Descriptive phrases: Littafi Mai Tsarki (Holy Book), Ruhu Mai Tsarki (Holy Spirit)
- Native terms: Uba (Father), Ubangiji (Lord), Jagora (leader/mentor)

**Compound Constructions:**
- Hausa frequently uses compound phrases:
  - Jibin Maraice na Ubangiji (Lord's Supper - lit: evening meal of Lord)
  - Mai bin Yesu (follower of Jesus - lit: one who follows Jesus)
  - Hanyoyin sadarwa (networks - lit: ways of communication)

### 4. Muslim-Background Believer Considerations
- Almajiri (disciple) originally referred to Quranic students
- Recontextualized for Christian discipleship
- Many terms resonate with Islamic religious education frameworks
- Creates bridges for understanding while requiring theological clarification

### 5. Northern Nigeria Context
- Hausa is lingua franca of northern Nigeria
- Predominantly Muslim region with Christian minority
- Christian terminology developed through mission history
- Multiple transliteration traditions (Portuguese, English, direct from Greek)
